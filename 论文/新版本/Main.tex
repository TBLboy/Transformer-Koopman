\title{Transformer--Koopman: History-Conditioned Scheduled Prediction and Stability-Guarded Frozen-LTV MPC}
\author{Bolin Tao}
\markboth{IEEE Journal Draft}{Tao: Transformer--Koopman}
\maketitle

{\color{blue}
\begin{abstract}
History-conditioned predictors can exploit information absent from instantaneous measurements, but their evolving scheduling coefficients generally depend on candidate inputs and destroy the convexity of nonlinear model predictive control (MPC). We develop a measurement-preserving temporal Transformer lift, recursively maintained measurement and input caches, and low-rank scheduled Koopman dynamics. One complete-model nominal rollout is used at each sample to freeze the coefficient sequence and formulate a strictly convex linear time-varying MPC quadratic program. For an output-reference-only interface, an internal nominal steady-input selector and a feasible first-input linear guard establish recursive feasibility and practical exponential tracking of the \emph{actual re-encoded closed loop} under explicit common-contraction, reference-feasibility, and residual assumptions. Exact exponential convergence holds for constant references corresponding to complete-model equilibria with zero re-encoding residual. Separately, a finite-horizon bound quantifies the optimization loss due to freezing relative to the complete learned predictor under the \emph{same} guard. An analytically certified nonzero-scheduling example demonstrates that the sufficient conditions are nonempty; the guarantees are conditional and do not automatically certify independently trained Transformer weights.
\end{abstract}
\begin{IEEEkeywords}
Koopman modeling, history-conditioned dynamics, temporal Transformer, linear time-varying MPC, quadratic programming, output tracking, contraction analysis.
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
Model predictive control (MPC) selects control inputs by comparing their predicted effects over a finite horizon. Its prediction model must therefore capture the response of a nonlinear system to candidate inputs while remaining tractable for repeated online optimization. When the model is identified from input--output data, these requirements concern both the information used to initialize predictions and the structure used to propagate them \cite{ljung1999system,nelles2020nonlinear,forgione2021dynonet}. An informative representation is needed to distinguish different dynamical conditions, whereas a control-oriented evolution model is needed to evaluate their response to future inputs.

Koopman-based prediction offers a way to impose such an evolution structure: nonlinear observables provide lifted coordinates in which a matrix model approximates the dynamics. The underlying Koopman operator is linear but generally infinite-dimensional \cite{koopman1931hamiltonian,mezic2005spectral,brunton2022modern}; a computational predictor must instead use a finite set of observables. Extended dynamic mode decomposition (EDMD) estimates the corresponding finite-dimensional operator approximation from data \cite{williams2015data}. For control, the additional task is to represent the effect of externally chosen inputs. Dynamic mode decomposition with control separates state and input contributions, while controlled EDMD constructions yield lifted predictors with an explicit input channel \cite{proctor2016dmdc,korda2018linear}. With a linear input-affine predictor, quadratic costs and linear constraints can then lead to a convex MPC problem. This control advantage motivates the structure, but its usefulness still depends on finding suitable observables. Neural lifting methods address that choice by learning the coordinates together with their evolution rather than fixing the entire dictionary beforehand \cite{takeishi2017koopman,lusch2018deep,otto2019linearly,han2020deep}.

Learning the observables, however, cannot recover information that is absent from their inputs. If different physical states produce the same current measurement but different subsequent responses, a lift of that measurement alone cannot distinguish them. Past measurements and applied inputs can supply additional information about the preceding evolution. This motivates history-based representations: delay-coordinate approaches augment observables with past measurements \cite{kamb2020timedelay,das2019delay}, and recurrent or temporal autoencoders learn features from sequences for prediction and control \cite{han2024robust,shenoy2025temporal}. Transformer-based lifting extends sequence feature extraction to Koopman-inspired forecasting, as in DeepKoopFormer \cite{forootani2026deepkoopformer}. These approaches motivate using history to initialize the predictor; they do not require the physical state to be reconstructed explicitly.

History can also condition the evolution model itself. While a fixed transition matrix offers a simple prediction structure, allowing its coefficients to depend on recent data provides additional modeling flexibility. Koopa employs context-dependent operators for nonstationary forecasting \cite{liu2023koopa}, while MamKO generates matrices from historical data for modeling and predictive control \cite{li2025mamko}. These studies motivate distinguishing history-dependent initialization from history-conditioned state propagation. In receding-horizon prediction, however, the scheduling history must evolve with the predicted trajectory. The resulting coefficients depend on predicted measurements and candidate inputs, so conditional linearity at a fixed context does not preserve convexity of the full MPC problem.

\begingroup\color{blue}
To retain a quadratic program (QP), the coefficient sequence can be computed along a nominal rollout and held fixed while optimizing the candidate inputs. Quasi-linear parameter-varying MPC similarly exploits fixed scheduling sequences to form tractable subproblems, including iterative QP schemes \cite{cisneros2016qlmpc,cisneros2020qlpv}. Here, by contrast, one complete-model nominal rollout and one frozen QP are performed \emph{per sampling instant}, with no iterative re-freezing. The resulting optimizer cannot be analyzed by silently treating its predicted trajectories as trajectories of the full learned model. Nor does the decrease of an objective defined by one frozen coefficient sequence imply a decrease of the distinct objective formed at the next sample; real-time iteration results also require their own algorithm-specific hypotheses \cite{diehl2005realtime,gros2020rti}.

A separate difficulty arises at the reference interface. Standard tracking formulations may assume a dynamically admissible lifted reference state and a compatible reference input sequence. These quantities are generally not available when an operator specifies only a measured-output setpoint or output trajectory. Even a nominally reachable output reference need not define an equilibrium of a history-scheduled model. The analysis must distinguish the internal reference-selection error, time variation of the requested output, re-encoding residuals, and the approximation introduced by freezing. In particular, a bound following only from compact state and input sets is not a meaningful tracking guarantee.

This paper develops a recursive history-conditioned Transformer--Koopman predictor and a \emph{stability-guarded}, single-freeze MPC controller. The predictor combines a measurement-preserving temporal lift with carried measurement and input histories, while low-rank schedules modulate the transition and input channel. For control, a right inverse of the nominal DC output map constructs a feasible steady-input candidate directly from the supplied output reference. A linear first-input guard limits the deviation from that candidate in proportion to the measured augmented-state tracking error. Consequently, closed-loop contraction can be proved using the \emph{implemented} input, without requiring a Lyapunov decrease between successive frozen optimization problems. The finite-horizon cost of freezing is analyzed independently, over the same guard-constrained feasible set.

The contributions are summarized as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Recursive history-conditioned Koopman prediction:} A measurement-preserving Transformer lift, nonredundant history caches, and low-rank scheduled dynamics explicitly distinguish history-based initialization from history-conditioned recursive evolution.
\item \emph{A certifiable scheduled model class:} A Schur nominal skeleton and computable perturbation, input-gain, and radius budgets provide sufficient common-contraction and invariance conditions compatible with nonzero scheduling. Prediction residuals remain an explicit, separately verified interface with the physical system.
\item \emph{An output-reference-only, single-freeze controller:} A nominal DC steady-input selector, one nonlinear nominal rollout, a strictly convex frozen-LTV QP, and an always-feasible first-input guard require neither external lifted reference trajectories nor external reference inputs.
\item \emph{Two distinct guarantees:} The actual re-encoded closed loop enjoys conditional practical exponential output tracking, with zero-error exponential convergence at exact complete-model equilibria under zero residual. An independent bound quantifies the finite-horizon optimization gap between frozen and complete learned-model predictions \emph{under the same guard}; it does not assert plant-level optimality or improvement over nominal steady-input control.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Preliminaries and Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:prelim}
\subsection{Koopman-Based Prediction}
\label{subsec:koopman_prelim}
Consider the discrete-time nonlinear system
\begin{equation}
\vs_{k+1}=f(\vs_k,\vu_k),\qquad \vx_k=h(\vs_k),
\label{eq:nonlinear_system}
\end{equation}
where $\vs_k\in\mathcal S\subseteq\R^{n_s}$ is the physical state and $\vu_k\in\R^m$ is the control input. The available measurement $\vx_k\in\R^n$ is obtained through the measurement map $h:\mathcal S\to\R^n$. The state transition map $f:\mathcal S\times\R^m\to\mathcal S$ governs the evolution of $\vs_k$ under $\vu_k$. Only $\vx_k$ is available to the predictor and the controller; we therefore learn the predictor from measured input--output trajectories and use finite observation histories to capture temporal information not resolved by individual measurements.

For a fixed input $\vu$, the controlled Koopman operator acts on an observable $\varphi:\mathcal S\to\R$ as \cite{koopman1931hamiltonian,mezic2005spectral,brunton2022modern}
\begin{equation}
(\mathcal K_{\vu}\varphi)(\vs)=\varphi(f(\vs,\vu)).
\label{eq:koopman_operator}
\end{equation}
The operator is linear with respect to observables:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal K_{\vu}(\alpha\varphi+\beta\psi)=\alpha\mathcal K_{\vu}\varphi+\beta\mathcal K_{\vu}\psi.
\label{eq:koopman_linearity}
\end{equation}

Finite-dimensional Koopman models approximate this evolution using a selected set of observables. Since only $\vx=h(\vs)$ is measured, we construct the observables from $\vx$; each component $g_j(\vx)$ corresponds to the physical-state observable $g_j\circ h$. An instantaneous lifting map takes the form
\begin{equation}
\vz_k=g(\vx_k)=[g_1(\vx_k),\ldots,g_d(\vx_k)]^\top\in\R^d,
\label{eq:instantaneous_lifting}
\end{equation}
with the input-affine lifted approximation
\begin{equation}
\vz_{k+1}\approx\mA\vz_k+\mB\vu_k.
\label{eq:linear_lifted}
\end{equation}
Here $\mA\in\R^{d\times d}$ approximates the evolution of the selected observables, and $\mB\in\R^{d\times m}$ represents the control-input contribution. Together they define a finite-dimensional approximation of the controlled Koopman evolution in the chosen lifted coordinates.

To incorporate temporal information, we collect the $P_h$ most recent measurements into the measurement-history window
\begin{equation}
\mX_k=\col(\vx_{k-P_h+1},\ldots,\vx_k)\in\R^{P_h n},
\label{eq:measurement_window}
\end{equation}
where $P_h\ge2$ is the window length and $\col(\cdot)$ denotes vertical stacking. We extend the lifting map to
\begin{equation}
\vz_k=G_\theta(\mX_k)=\col(\vx_k,\psi_\theta(\mX_k))\in\R^d,
\label{eq:state_preserving_lifting}
\end{equation}
where $d=n+n_\psi$. The lift retains the current measurement $\vx_k$ as its leading block, so the readout is fixed:
\begin{equation}
\vx_k=C_x\vz_k,\qquad C_x=[I_n\ 0].
\label{eq:state_readout}
\end{equation}
This representation incorporates recent measurements into the lifted state. The transition model may additionally depend on the recent trajectory context, as developed in Section~\ref{sec:model}.

\subsection{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:problem}
The available training data comprise recorded measurement--input trajectories
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal D &= \{\tau^{(j)}\}_{j=1}^{N_{\rm tr}},\\
\tau^{(j)} &= \{(\vx_t^{(j)},\vu_t^{(j)})\}_{t=0}^{T_j},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:training_data}
\end{equation}
where $N_{\rm tr}$ is the number of recorded trajectories and $T_j$ is the last time index of the $j$th trajectory.
For each admissible index $k$, the measured window $\mX_k$ is defined in~\eqref{eq:measurement_window}, and the past-input history is
\begin{equation}
v_k = \col(\vu_{k-P_h+1},\ldots,\vu_{k-1}) \in \R^{(P_h-1)m}.
\label{eq:past_input_history}
\end{equation}
The current input $\vu_k$ is applied after the measured window and is therefore not part of $v_k$.

Given the measured window $\mX_k$, the past-input history $v_k$, and a candidate input sequence $\vu_{k:k+N-1}$, the prediction task is to estimate $\vx_{k+1},\ldots,\vx_{k+N}$, where $N\ge1$ is the prediction horizon, which is also the horizon of the receding-horizon controller in Section~\ref{sec:control}. The predictor should exploit the measured window to initialize the lifted representation and support recursive multi-step prediction. The learned predictor is then used for reference tracking subject to input constraints $\vu_k\in\mathcal U$, where $\mathcal U=\{u:H_uu\leq h_u\}$ is a nonempty compact convex polytope.

The objective of this work is to develop a history-conditioned lifted predictor and a computationally tractable receding-horizon controller, and to establish a practical exponential tracking guarantee for the implemented closed loop under suitable conditions.

\section{History-Conditioned Scheduled Koopman Modeling}
\label{sec:model}
\subsection{History-Conditioned Koopman Predictor}
\label{sec:temporal_lifting}
To construct the history-conditioned predictor formulated in Section~\ref{sec:prelim}, we first specify the temporal encoder used in the lifting map $G_\theta$ in~\eqref{eq:state_preserving_lifting}. Given the measured window $\mX_k$ in~\eqref{eq:measurement_window}, arrange its successive measurement blocks into a $P_h\times n$ array and partition it into $N_p=P_h/p$ nonoverlapping temporal patches of length $p$, where $p$ divides $P_h$. Let $\mX_k^{(j)}\in\R^{p\times n}$ denote the $j$th patch. Each patch is mapped to a token through a shared affine embedding,
\begin{equation}
\vphi_k^{(j)}=\mW_p\operatorname{vec}(\mX_k^{(j)})+\vb_p\in\R^{d_{\rm model}},
\label{eq:patch_embedding}
\end{equation}
where $j=1,\ldots,N_p$, and $\operatorname{vec}(\mX_k^{(j)})\in\R^{pn}$ stacks the $p$ measurement vectors in the $j$th patch into a single column. The embedding parameters $\mW_p\in\R^{d_{\rm model}\times pn}$ and $\vb_p\in\R^{d_{\rm model}}$ are shared across patches. Sinusoidal positional encodings $\vp^{(j)}\in\R^{d_{\rm model}}$ \cite{vaswani2017attention} are added to retain temporal order. An $L_T$-layer Transformer encoder then produces
\begin{equation}
\mH_k=\operatorname{TransformerEncoder}\left(\{\vphi_k^{(j)}+\vp^{(j)}\}_{j=1}^{N_p}\right).
\label{eq:transformer_encoder}
\end{equation}
Here $\mH_k\in\R^{N_p\times d_{\rm model}}$ contains the contextualized patch representations, with $\mH_k^{(j)}\in\R^{d_{\rm model}}$ denoting the representation of the $j$th patch. Mean pooling followed by a linear projection gives
\begin{equation}
\psi_\theta(\mX_k)=\mW_h\left(\frac1{N_p}\sum_{j=1}^{N_p}\mH_k^{(j)}\right)+\vb_h,
\label{eq:history_features}
\end{equation}
where $\psi_\theta(\mX_k)\in\R^{d-n}$, $\mW_h\in\R^{(d-n)\times d_{\rm model}}$, and $\vb_h\in\R^{d-n}$. Together with~\eqref{eq:state_preserving_lifting}, this specifies the history-dependent lifting map $G_\theta$, which initializes the lifted state from the measured window. Recursive multi-step prediction, however, requires two further components: a scheduling history that is carried along the predicted trajectory, so that the transition remains conditioned on history beyond the initial sample, and a transition model whose coefficients depend on this history. We carry the measurement and input histories in an augmented state and let them enter the transition through low-rank corrections to a shared nominal model. The following paragraphs specify these components, and Definition~\ref{def:history_scheduled_predictor} collects them into the recursive predictor.

To carry the scheduling history along the predicted trajectory, define the measurement cache and the augmented state
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
a_k&=\col(\vx_{k-P_h+1},\ldots,\vx_{k-1}),\\
q_k&=\col(\vz_k,a_k,v_k)\in\R^{s_q},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:augmented_state}
\end{equation}
where the measurement cache $a_k$ collects the $P_h-1$ past measurements of the measured window $\mX_k$ in~\eqref{eq:measurement_window}, omitting the current $\vx_k$ already carried in $\vz_k$, and $v_k$ is the past-input history~\eqref{eq:past_input_history}. The augmented state $q_k$ stacks the lifted state with both caches and has dimension $s_q=d+(P_h-1)(n+m)$. For $q=\col(\vz,a,v)$, the reconstructed window is $\mX(q)=\col(a,C_x\vz)\in\R^{P_hn}$, where $C_x\vz$ recovers the current measurement from the lifted state through~\eqref{eq:state_readout}, and the scheduling context is
\begin{equation}
\vc(q)=C_\phi\bigl(\mX(q),v\bigr)\in\R^{d_c},
\label{eq:recursive_context}
\end{equation}
where $C_\phi:\R^{P_hn+(P_h-1)m}\to\R^{d_c}$ is the context encoder. At a measured sample, where $\vz_k=G_\theta(\mX_k)$ and the caches are taken from recorded data, $\mX(q_k)=\mX_k$ by~\eqref{eq:state_readout}; along a predicted trajectory, the reconstructed window is instead formed from the propagated state, so that $\vc$ remains a function of the state that $F$ propagates rather than an external signal. Thus, the scheduling context depends on the reconstructed window and past inputs, but not on the current decision input.

The scheduled transition and input matrices are
\begin{align}
\mA(\vc)&=\mA_0+\mU_A\operatorname{diag}(\gamma_A(\vc))\mV_A^\top,
\label{eq:scheduled_A}\\
\mB(\vx)&=\mB_0+\mU_B\operatorname{diag}(\gamma_B(\vx))\mV_B^\top,
\label{eq:scheduled_B}
\end{align}
where $\mA_0\in\R^{d\times d}$ and $\mB_0\in\R^{d\times m}$ are the nominal lifted-state transition and input matrices, respectively. The operator $\operatorname{diag}(\cdot)$ forms a diagonal matrix from a vector. The matrices $\mU_A,\mV_A\in\R^{d\times r_A}$ and $\mU_B\in\R^{d\times r_B}$, $\mV_B\in\R^{m\times r_B}$ are learnable low-rank factors that specify the correction directions for $\mA_0$ and $\mB_0$, with $r_A$ and $r_B$ denoting the respective rank bounds of the corrections. The scheduling vectors $\gamma_A(\vc)\in\R^{r_A}$ and $\gamma_B(\vx)\in\R^{r_B}$, evaluated at the context $\vc$ and the current measurement $\vx=C_x\vz$, determine the signed magnitudes of the corresponding rank-one correction components. The scheduling functions are parameterized by
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\gamma_{\chi,j}(y)={}&g_\chi\left[\operatorname{softmax}(W_\chi^\pi y+b_\chi^\pi)\right]_j\\
&{}\cdot\tanh\left([W_\chi y+b_\chi]_j\right),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:scheduling_function}
\end{equation}
where $\chi\in\{A,B\}$ indexes the scheduling branch, $j=1,\ldots,r_\chi$, and $y=\vc$ for $\chi=A$ or $y=\vx$ for $\chi=B$. The matrices $W_\chi^\pi,W_\chi\in\R^{r_\chi\times d_y}$ and vectors $b_\chi^\pi,b_\chi\in\R^{r_\chi}$ are learnable parameters, with $d_y=d_c$ for $\chi=A$ and $d_y=n$ for $\chi=B$. The softmax term distributes the scheduling weights across the low-rank components, while the hyperbolic tangent determines their signed modulation. The nonnegative gain $g_\chi$ bounds the magnitude of each scheduling coefficient. If each factor column has Euclidean norm at most one, it follows that $\norm{\mA(\vc)-\mA_0}_2\leq g_A$ and $\norm{\mB(\vx)-\mB_0}_2\leq g_B$. Here $\norm{\cdot}_2$ denotes the spectral norm for matrices and the Euclidean norm for vectors. The gains $g_A$ and $g_B$ therefore serve as explicit correction budgets on the scheduled model.

\begin{definition}[History-Conditioned Scheduled Koopman Predictor]
\label{def:history_scheduled_predictor}
Let $G_\theta$ be the lifting map~\eqref{eq:state_preserving_lifting}, let $\vc(\cdot)$ be the recursive context~\eqref{eq:recursive_context}, and let $\mA(\cdot)$ and $\mB(\cdot)$ be the scheduled matrices~\eqref{eq:scheduled_A}--\eqref{eq:scheduling_function}. The history-conditioned scheduled Koopman predictor initializes the augmented state from the observed histories as
\begin{equation}
q_k^E=\col(G_\theta(\mX_k),a_k,v_k),
\label{eq:encoded_initialization}
\end{equation}
where $a_k$ is the measurement cache defined in~\eqref{eq:augmented_state} and $v_k$ is the past-input history defined in~\eqref{eq:past_input_history}. The superscript $E$ denotes an encoder-initialized state, and we will use $P$ to denote model-propagated states in what follows. Starting from $q_k^E$, the augmented state is propagated according to $q^+=F(q,\vu)$, where $F:\R^{s_q}\times\R^m\to\R^{s_q}$ is defined by
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
F(q,\vu)=\col\bigl(&\mA(\vc(q))\vz+\mB(C_x\vz)\vu,\\
&S_xa+E_xC_x\vz,\ S_uv+E_u\vu\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:recursive_predictor}
\end{equation}
The first block propagates the lifted state, while the remaining blocks update the histories used for subsequent scheduling: $S_x$ and $S_u$ discard the oldest entry of the respective cache, and $E_x$ and $E_u$ append the current measurement $C_x\vz$ and the applied input $\vu$, so that the next context $\vc(q^+)$ is evaluated on the predicted window.\envend
\end{definition}

The encoder is evaluated once per control sample to form $q_k^E$. Predictions over the horizon are then obtained by iterating $F$ on this initial state, without re-encoding the predicted windows. Consequently, the model-propagated state need not coincide with the state encoded from the next measurement. This discrepancy is written as
\begin{equation}
q_{k+1}^E=F(q_k^E,\vu_k)+w_k.
\label{eq:reencoding_residual}
\end{equation}
The cache blocks of $F$ reproduce exact bookkeeping of applied inputs and measurements, so the residual satisfies $w_k=\col(r_k,0,0)$ and lies entirely in the lifted block. It captures two sources of discrepancy: the one-step measurement prediction error and the mismatch between the propagated features and the encoder output on the realized window. The explicit decomposition is provided in Appendix~\ref{app:construction}.

\subsection{Model Learning and Contractive Design}
\label{sec:training}
We train the predictor of Section~\ref{sec:temporal_lifting} in two stages. One-step prediction first fits the lifting map and nominal dynamics. Recursive rollouts then train the scheduled model over the prediction horizon used by the controller.

In the first stage, both scheduling corrections are disabled. We jointly optimize $G_\theta$, $\mA_0$, and $\mB_0$, with $\mA_0$ constrained to be Schur stable. The nominal prediction $\widetilde\vz_{k+1}^0=\mA_0\vz_k^E+\mB_0\vu_k$ is fitted by minimizing
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\mathcal L_{\rm init}=\sum_{k\in\mathcal I_1}\bigl[&
\norm{C_x\widetilde\vz_{k+1}^0-\vx_{k+1}}_2^2\\
&+\alpha_0\norm{\widetilde\vz_{k+1}^0-\vz_{k+1}^E}_2^2\bigr],
\end{split}
\label{eq:training_one_step_objects}
\end{equation}
where $\alpha_0>0$ is a weighting coefficient and $\mathcal I_1$ indexes one-step samples $(\mX_k,\vu_k,\mX_{k+1})$ drawn from individual recorded trajectories.

In the second stage, $\mA_0$ and $\mB_0$ are fixed, and both schedules are activated. We jointly train the lifting map, context encoder, low-rank factors, and scheduling heads under the constraints specified below. Initialize $q_{k|k}^P=q_k^E$ from recorded histories and propagate $q_{k+i+1|k}^P=F(q_{k+i|k}^P,\vu_{k+i})$ for $i=0,\ldots,H_R-1$. We set $H_R=N$ so that training and control use the same prediction horizon. With $\vz_{k+i|k}^P$ denoting the lifted-state block of $q_{k+i|k}^P$, minimize
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\mathcal L_R=\sum_{k\in\mathcal I_R}\sum_{i=1}^{H_R}\omega_i\bigl[&
\norm{C_x\vz_{k+i|k}^P-\vx_{k+i}}_2^2\\
&+\alpha_R\norm{\vz_{k+i|k}^P-\vz_{k+i}^E}_2^2\bigr],
\end{split}
\label{eq:joint_training_loss}
\end{equation}
where $\omega_i=1/H_R$ and $\alpha_R>0$. The set $\mathcal I_R$ indexes multi-step samples comprising the initial histories $(\mX_k,v_k)$, recorded inputs $\vu_{k:k+H_R-1}$, and subsequent measurements $\vx_{k+1},\ldots,\vx_{k+H_R}$ from a single trajectory. The prediction branch uses $G_\theta$ only at initialization; subsequent contexts use reconstructed windows and recorded inputs. The target states are encoded from measured windows formed by successively shifting $\mX_k$ and appending these measurements. In both stages, gradients pass through every encoder evaluation in the losses, and $C_x$ remains fixed. The deployed predictor is deterministic, with dropout disabled and normalization fixed.

In the second stage, we constrain the scheduled corrections using a fixed contraction metric. With the corrections disabled, the augmented predictor has the state matrix
\begin{equation}
M_0=\begin{bmatrix}\mA_0&0&0\\E_xC_x&S_x&0\\0&0&S_u\end{bmatrix}.
\label{eq:lyapunov_skeleton}
\end{equation}
Since $\mA_0$ is Schur stable and the cache shifts $S_x,S_u$ are nilpotent, $M_0$ is Schur stable. We therefore choose $\Pi\succ0$ as the unique solution of the discrete Lyapunov equation
\begin{equation*}
M_0^T\Pi M_0-\Pi=-I.
\end{equation*}
Both $M_0$ and $\Pi$ remain fixed throughout the second stage.

The fixed metric $\Pi$ defines the weighted squared norm $\norm{e}_\Pi^2=e^\top\Pi e$ and the analysis domain $\mathcal Q=\{q:\norm{q}_\Pi\le M\}$, where $M>0$. The induced matrix norm $\norm{L}_\Pi=\norm{\Pi^{1/2}L\Pi^{-1/2}}_2$ gives the nominal contraction rate $\lambda_0=\norm{M_0}_\Pi<1$. On $\mathcal Q$, let $L_c$ bound the Euclidean derivative of the context map $q\mapsto\vc(q)$. For each scheduling branch $\chi\in\{A,B\}$, define
\begin{equation}
\kappa_\chi=\norm{W_\chi}_2+\tfrac12\sqrt{r_\chi}\norm{W_\chi^\pi}_2,\qquad L_\chi=g_\chi\kappa_\chi.
\label{eq:Lipschitz_constants}
\end{equation}
During the second stage, we constrain each factor column to have Euclidean norm at most one and enforce $\kappa_\chi\le\bar\kappa_\chi$ and $L_c\le\bar L_c$ uniformly over the allowed parameters on $\mathcal Q$. Using these fixed finite bounds, we select $g_A,g_B$ by~\eqref{eq:gain_choice} and keep them fixed. Appendix~\ref{app:construction} provides sufficient head-norm, MLP, and attention bounds for enforcing these constraints.

\begin{proposition}[Constructible contractive model class]
\label{prop:contractive_class}
Fix a Schur skeleton~\eqref{eq:lyapunov_skeleton}, $\mB_0$, a compact input polytope $\mathcal U$, and a trial ball $\mathcal Q$. Assume factor-column norms at most one and uniform bounds $\bar\kappa_\chi,\bar L_c$ over the allowed scheduling parameters. For a chosen lift and measured system, assume $\norm{w_t}_\Pi\le\bar w$ for every measured transition with $q_t^E\in\mathcal Q$ and $\vu_t\in\mathcal U$. Let $b_0^{\rm drv}$ bound the nominal input drive. If the ball covers the initial encoded-state set and
\begin{equation}
\Delta=(1-\lambda_0)M-b_0^{\rm drv}-\bar w>0,
\label{eq:radius_margin}
\end{equation}
then the explicit positive-gain choices in \eqref{eq:gain_choice} yield uniform same-input contraction of the complete model and every frozen model generated by a nominal point in $\mathcal Q$, with common bounds over the allowed scheduling parameters. Their rates $\lambda,\lambda_{\rm fr}<1$ and drive bound $b_u$ satisfy
\begin{equation}
\lambda M+b_u+\bar w<M,\qquad \lambda_{\rm fr}M+b_u<M.
\label{eq:invariant_radius}
\end{equation}
Both modulation gains can be nonzero. Nonconstant heads and effective factor directions give genuinely history-dependent members of the class.

For $q_{k|k}^P=q_k^E\in\mathcal Q$ and identical prescribed inputs in $\mathcal U$, the prediction error satisfies
\begin{equation*}
\norm{q_{k+i}^E-q_{k+i|k}^P}_\Pi\le\bar w\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}\lambda^j,\qquad i\ge1.
\end{equation*}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Schur stability gives the common Lyapunov norm. The full derivative perturbation includes the direct correction, the derivative of the history schedule acting on $\vz$, and the derivative of $\mB(C_x\vz)\vu$. Their bounds give $\norm{D_qF-M_0}_\Pi\le\nu_{\rm bd}$; the frozen bound omits the schedule derivatives. Using $\kappa_\chi\le\bar\kappa_\chi$ and $L_c\le\bar L_c$ makes the bounds uniform over the allowed parameters. With the ball and these upper bounds fixed, the perturbation budgets are linear in the common gain scale. The strict choice \eqref{eq:gain_choice} leaves positive contraction and radius margins. The radius inequalities first keep the complete and frozen predictions in $\mathcal Q$. The residual bound then keeps the next encoded state in $\mathcal Q$, so domain invariance follows by induction. The detailed bounds and a nonconstant, encoding-consistent periodic example are given in Appendix~\ref{app:construction}.

For identical prescribed inputs, combining~\eqref{eq:reencoding_residual} with same-input contraction bounds each next prediction error by $\lambda$ times the current error plus $\bar w$. Iterating from zero initial prediction error yields the stated bound.
\end{proof}

\section{Stability-Guarded Frozen-LTV Model Predictive Control}
\label{sec:control}
\subsection{Output-Reference-Based Single-Freeze QP}
\label{subsec:frozen_mpc}
The controller receives only an output reference $\vr_k\in\R^{p_y}$, rather than an augmented reference state or a reference input trajectory. Let $\vy_k=Cq_k^E$, where
$C=C_yC_xE_z^T\in\R^{p_y\times s_q}$ extracts the controlled output and $E_z^Tq=\vz$. A preview $\vr_k,\ldots,\vr_{k+N}$ is used in the finite-horizon cost. We take $P_h\ge2$ so that the existing input-history cache contains the immediately preceding applied input; $E_{\rm prev}q\in\R^m$ extracts this entry.

The nominal augmented skeleton in~\eqref{eq:lyapunov_skeleton} has input matrix
\begin{equation}
D_0=\col(\mB_0,0,E_u),\qquad F_0(q,\vu)=M_0q+D_0\vu.
\label{eq:nominal_input_matrix}
\end{equation}
Since $M_0$ is Schur, $I-M_0$ is nonsingular. Define its DC output gain and a fixed right inverse by
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
G_0&=C(I-M_0)^{-1}D_0,\\
H_b&=G_0^T(G_0G_0^T)^{-1},\qquad G_0H_b=I_{p_y},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:reference_selector}
\end{equation}
provided $\operatorname{rank}(G_0)=p_y$. From the supplied output reference, the controller internally constructs
\begin{equation}
\vu_k^b=H_b\vr_k,\quad q_k^b=T_b\vr_k,\quad
T_b=(I-M_0)^{-1}D_0H_b.
\label{eq:reference_equilibrium}
\end{equation}
Thus $Cq_k^b=\vr_k$, $F_0(q_k^b,\vu_k^b)=q_k^b$, and $E_{\rm prev}q_k^b=\vu_k^b$. The pair $(q_k^b,\vu_k^b)$ is an \emph{algebraic nominal steady pair}, not an assumed trajectory of the complete scheduled predictor. In particular, for a moving reference it need not satisfy $q_{k+1}^b=F(q_k^b,\vu_k^b)$. The admissible output reference class is specified below.

At time $k$, initialize $q_k^E$ by~\eqref{eq:encoded_initialization}, and choose a nominal sequence $\bar U_k=\col(\bar\vu_{0|k},\ldots,\bar\vu_{N-1|k})\in\mathcal U^N$. Perform \emph{one} complete-model nominal rollout,
\begin{equation}
\bar q_{0|k}=q_k^E,\qquad
\bar q_{i+1|k}=F(\bar q_{i|k},\bar\vu_{i|k}),\quad 0\le i<N.
\label{eq:nominal_rollout}
\end{equation}
Freeze the scheduled matrices $\bar\mA_{i|k}=\mA(\vc(\bar q_{i|k}))$ and $\bar\mB_{i|k}=\mB(C_xE_z^T\bar q_{i|k})$. Each candidate input sequence then generates the affine prediction
\begin{align}
p_{0|k}&=q_k^E,\qquad
p_{i+1|k}=M_{i|k}p_{i|k}+D_{i|k}\vu_{i|k},
\label{eq:frozen_model}\\[-2pt]
M_{i|k}&=\begin{bmatrix}
\bar\mA_{i|k}&0&0\\E_xC_x&S_x&0\\0&0&S_u
\end{bmatrix},\quad
D_{i|k}=\col(\bar\mB_{i|k},0,E_u).\nonumber
\end{align}
For the nominal candidate, $p_{i|k}(\bar U_k)=\bar q_{i|k}$ for all $i$. This is coefficient freezing, not a Jacobian linearization of the complete recursion. In particular, its first step is exact for any candidate first input:
\begin{equation}
p_{1|k}=F(q_k^E,\vu_{0|k}).
\label{eq:exact_first_step}
\end{equation}

For $Q_y\succ0$, $Q_f\succeq0$, $S\succ0$, $\beta\ge0$, and an optional anchoring weight $\omega>0$, define
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
J_k^{\rm fr}(U;\bar U_k)={}&\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\bigl(
\norm{Cp_{i|k}-\vr_{k+i}}_{Q_y}^2\\[-2pt]
&\qquad+\norm{\vu_{i|k}-E_{\rm prev}p_{i|k}}_S^2\bigr)\\
&+\beta\norm{Cp_{N|k}-\vr_{k+N}}_{Q_f}^2\\
&+\omega\norm{\vu_{0|k}-\vu_k^b}_2^2,
\end{split}
\label{eq:stage_cost}
\end{equation}
where $\norm{v}_W^2=v^TWv$. To ensure stability \emph{independently} of the optimality of the returned input, set $E_k=\norm{q_k^E-q_k^b}_\Pi$ and impose the first-input guard
\begin{equation}
-\kappa E_k\mathbf1_m\le\vu_{0|k}-\vu_k^b
\le\kappa E_k\mathbf1_m,
\label{eq:stability_guard}
\end{equation}
with design constant $\kappa\ge0$. At the current sample $E_k$ and $\vu_k^b$ are known, so these inequalities are \emph{linear} in the decision variables. The frozen MPC problem is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\min_{U}\quad &J_k^{\rm fr}(U;\bar U_k)\\
\text{s.t.}\quad &p_{0|k}=q_k^E,\\
&p_{i+1|k}=M_{i|k}p_{i|k}+D_{i|k}\vu_{i|k},\\
&\vu_{i|k}\in\mathcal U,\quad 0\le i<N,\\
&\text{guard }\eqref{eq:stability_guard}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:quadratic_template}
\end{equation}
Eliminating the affine state recursions leaves a single strictly convex QP in $U$. Its \emph{current-sample} feasible set is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal F_k=\{U\in\mathcal U^N:
\norm{\vu_{0|k}-\vu_k^b}_\infty\le\kappa E_k\}.
\label{eq:guarded_feasible_set}
\end{equation}
The analysis ball $\mathcal Q$ is not imposed as an online state constraint. The optional anchoring term encourages the feasible input toward $\vu_k^b$ but is \emph{not} the source of the closed-loop stability condition; the guard is.

For a primal-feasible QP return $\widehat U_k\in\mathcal F_k$, implement only its first input and update the nominal \emph{freezing} sequence by
\begin{align}
\vu_k&=\widehat\vu_{0|k},\label{eq:apply_first}\\
\bar U_{k+1}&=\col(\widehat\vu_{1|k},\ldots,
\widehat\vu_{N-1|k},\widehat\vu_{N-1|k}).
\label{eq:nominal_shift}
\end{align}
For $N=1$, set $\bar U_{k+1}=\widehat U_k$. The shifted nominal is only required to belong to $\mathcal U^N$; it need \emph{not} satisfy the \emph{next} sample's new guard. Next-sample QP feasibility follows separately from the steady-input selector, as established below.

\begin{algorithm}[!t]
\caption{Output-Reference Stability-Guarded Single-Freeze MPC}
\label{alg:mpc}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE \textbf{Given:} $F,\Pi,\mathcal U,N,Q_y,Q_f,S,\beta,\omega,\kappa,H_b,T_b$.
\STATE \textbf{Initialize:} any $\bar U_0\in\mathcal U^N$.
\FOR{$k=0,1,\ldots$}
\STATE Measure and re-encode $q_k^E$; acquire the output-reference preview $\vr_{k:k+N}$.
\STATE Set $\vu_k^b=H_b\vr_k$, $q_k^b=T_b\vr_k$, and $E_k=\norm{q_k^E-q_k^b}_\Pi$.
\FOR{$i=0,\ldots,N-1$}
\STATE Evaluate $M_{i|k},D_{i|k}$ at $\bar q_{i|k}$ and propagate $\bar q_{i+1|k}$ with $F$.
\ENDFOR
\STATE Solve the single QP~\eqref{eq:quadratic_template} over $\mathcal F_k$ and obtain a \emph{primal-feasible} $\widehat U_k$.
\STATE Apply $\vu_k=\widehat\vu_{0|k}$ and update $\bar U_{k+1}$ by~\eqref{eq:nominal_shift}.
\ENDFOR
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Recursive Feasibility and Closed-Loop Tracking}
\label{subsec:closed_loop}
The contraction certificate of Proposition~\ref{prop:contractive_class} applies to the complete model and frozen families. For clarity, the deployed-model conditions needed below are made explicit.
\begin{assumption}[Common contraction and invariant domain]
\label{ass:domain}
Let $\mathcal Q=\{q:\norm{q}_\Pi\le M\}$ contain $q_0^E$. The input set $\mathcal U$ is a nonempty compact convex polytope. For all $q,q',\bar q\in\mathcal Q$ and $\vu\in\mathcal U$, the full predictor and every coefficient-frozen map $F_{\bar q}^{\rm fr}$ satisfy
\begin{align}
\norm{F(q,\vu)-F(q',\vu)}_\Pi&\le\lambda\norm{q-q'}_\Pi,\nonumber\\
\norm{F_{\bar q}^{\rm fr}(q,\vu)-F_{\bar q}^{\rm fr}(q',\vu)}_\Pi&\le\lambda_{\rm fr}\norm{q-q'}_\Pi,\nonumber\\
\norm{F(0,\vu)}_\Pi,\ \norm{F_{\bar q}^{\rm fr}(0,\vu)}_\Pi&\le b_u,\nonumber\\
\norm{F(q,\vu)-F_{\bar q}^{\rm fr}(q,\vu)}_\Pi&\le\bar\eta,
\label{eq:common_contraction}
\end{align}
where $\lambda,\lambda_{\rm fr}<1$. The actual re-encoding residual obeys $\norm{w_k}_\Pi\le\bar w$, and
\begin{equation}
\lambda M+b_u+\bar w\le M,\qquad
\lambda_{\rm fr}M+b_u\le M.
\label{eq:domain_radius}
\end{equation}
These bounds are uniform over admissible states, inputs, and frozen schedules.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[DC-feasible output references]
\label{ass:reference}
The nominal DC gain in~\eqref{eq:reference_selector} has full row rank. The supplied reference $\vr_k$ lies in
\begin{equation}
\mathcal R_{\rm adm}=\{r\in\R^{p_y}:H_br\in\mathcal U,
\ \norm{T_br}_\Pi\le M\}
\label{eq:reference_admissibility}
\end{equation}
for all $k$, with $\sup_k\norm{\vr_k}_2\le\bar r$. It has bounded variation $\sup_k\norm{\vr_{k+1}-\vr_k}_2\le\bar\nu_r$. Define the equilibrium defect and the nominal steady-pair variation by
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\bar d_b&=\sup_k\norm{F(q_k^b,\vu_k^b)-q_k^b}_\Pi,\\
\bar\nu_b&=\sup_k\norm{q_{k+1}^b-q_k^b}_\Pi.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:reference_budgets}
\end{equation}
These constants are bounded on the admissible class; in particular
$\bar\nu_b\le\norm{\Pi^{1/2}T_b}_2\bar\nu_r$.
\end{assumption}

Let $D(q)=\col(\mB(C_xE_z^Tq),0,E_u)$, so $F(q,u)-F(q,v)=D(q)(u-v)$. Define the finite input gain and output readout gain
\begin{equation}
L_u=\sup_{q\in\mathcal Q}\norm{\Pi^{1/2}D(q)}_2,\qquad
c_y=\norm{C\Pi^{-1/2}}_2.
\label{eq:input_output_gains}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Recursive feasibility and practical exponential output tracking]
\label{thm:tracking}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:domain}--\ref{ass:reference}, execute Algorithm~\ref{alg:mpc} with exact input application and a \emph{primal-feasible} returned sequence at each sample. Choose $\kappa\ge0$ so that
\begin{equation}
\rho_g:=\lambda+L_u\sqrt m\,\kappa<1.
\label{eq:guard_condition}
\end{equation}
Then the QP is recursively feasible, and all realized and model-predicted states remain in $\mathcal Q$. Set $d_g=\bar d_b+\bar\nu_b+\bar w$. For every $k\ge0$, the \emph{actual re-encoded closed loop} satisfies
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
E_k&\le\rho_g^k E_0+
\frac{1-\rho_g^k}{1-\rho_g}\,d_g,\\
\norm{\vy_k-\vr_k}_2&\le c_y\left(\rho_g^k E_0+
\frac{1-\rho_g^k}{1-\rho_g}\,d_g\right).
\end{split}
\label{eq:practical_tracking}
\end{equation}
If the reference is constant and $(q^b,\vu^b)$ is an exact complete-model equilibrium, $F(q^b,\vu^b)=q^b$, and $w_k=0$, the actual output error converges to zero exponentially, including for nonconstant scheduling laws. No QP optimal-value descent or certified objective gap is required for this tracking conclusion.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By Assumption~\ref{ass:reference}, $\vu_k^b\in\mathcal U$. Thus the candidate with $\vu_{0|k}=\vu_k^b$ and all later components equal to $\vu_k^b$ satisfies both the input constraints and guard~\eqref{eq:stability_guard}, irrespective of the nominal sequence; the current QP is nonempty. The shifted nominal in~\eqref{eq:nominal_shift} lies in $\mathcal U^N$, hence the next complete-model nominal rollout is well defined. Assumption~\ref{ass:domain}, applied first to nominal rollouts and frozen predictions and then to the actual transition with $w_k$, proves domain invariance inductively. In particular, it does not presume that the shifted nominal satisfies the next QP's guard.

The implemented guard and the input-affine structure give
\begin{align}
\norm{\vu_k-\vu_k^b}_2&\le\sqrt m\,\kappa E_k,\nonumber\\
\norm{F(q_k^E,\vu_k)-F(q_k^E,\vu_k^b)}_\Pi
&\le L_u\sqrt m\,\kappa E_k.
\end{align}
Using~\eqref{eq:reencoding_residual}, insert $F(q_k^E,\vu_k^b)$, $F(q_k^b,\vu_k^b)$, and $q_k^b$ between $q_{k+1}^E$ and $q_{k+1}^b$. Same-input contraction yields
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
E_{k+1}&\le(\lambda+L_u\sqrt m\,\kappa)E_k\\
&\quad+\norm{F(q_k^b,\vu_k^b)-q_k^b}_\Pi\\
&\quad+\norm{q_{k+1}^b-q_k^b}_\Pi+\norm{w_k}_\Pi\\
&\le\rho_gE_k+d_g.
\end{split}
\label{eq:tracking_recursion}
\end{equation}
The scalar geometric recursion and $Cq_k^b=\vr_k$ give~\eqref{eq:practical_tracking}. If $d_g=0$, the bound converges geometrically to zero. 
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Guarantee and scope]
\label{rem:tracking_scope}
The result establishes a \emph{closed-loop} practical tracking property, not merely bounded predictions; the only externally supplied reference is the output sequence $\vr$ (and its $N$-step preview). It does not guarantee tracking of arbitrary unreachable or rapidly varying outputs. The guard is sufficient and may constrain the freedom of the predictive optimizer; this theorem does not assert that MPC outperforms direct use of $\vu_k^b$. Full-schedule equilibrium consistency is needed for the ideal zero-radius statement: the nominal identity $F_0(q^b,\vu^b)=q^b$ alone does not imply $F(q^b,\vu^b)=q^b$. The prescribed $\omega>0$ is a \emph{performance tuning parameter}: because the proof relies only on primal feasibility of the first-input guard, changing $\omega$ alone does not improve the certified convergence rate $\rho_g$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Deployment-level certificate]
\label{rem:deployment}
The controller requires more than Schur stability of $M_0$. A deployed model must additionally verify the complete-model contraction bound $\lambda$, the invariant-domain and residual margins in~\eqref{eq:domain_radius}, the scheduled input gain $L_u$, the DC rank and feasible reference class in~\eqref{eq:reference_admissibility}, and $\rho_g<1$. The certified asymptotic output bound $c_y d_g/(1-\rho_g)$ is informative only when it improves on the a priori bound $c_yM+\bar r$. Appendix~\ref{app:estimates} gives conservative budgets for the scheduled input gain and equilibrium defect. These checks are not automatic consequences of training loss minimization.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Cost of Freezing the Learned Predictor}
\label{subsec:freeze_analysis}
The stability guard concerns the applied first input. An independent estimate quantifies the optimization price of freezing, \emph{under the same guard}. For any fixed $U\in\mathcal F_k$, define the complete learned-model trajectory
\begin{equation}
f_{0|k}=q_k^E,\qquad f_{i+1|k}=F(f_{i|k},\vu_{i|k}),
\label{eq:full_candidate}
\end{equation}
and let $J_k^F(U)$ denote exactly the cost in~\eqref{eq:stage_cost} evaluated at $f_{i|k}$ instead of $p_{i|k}$. This is an analytical benchmark; the online algorithm does not optimize $J_k^F$.

Set $\mathsf D_0=\mathsf D_1=0$ and, for $2\le i\le N$,
\begin{equation}
\mathsf D_i=\bar\eta\sum_{j=0}^{i-2}\lambda^j.
\label{eq:frozen_discrepancy}
\end{equation}
For the output and terminal weights define
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
a_y&=\norm{Q_y^{1/2}C\Pi^{-1/2}}_2,\\
a_f&=\norm{Q_f^{1/2}C\Pi^{-1/2}}_2,\\
h_y&=a_yM+\norm{Q_y^{1/2}}_2\bar r,\\
h_f&=a_fM+\norm{Q_f^{1/2}}_2\bar r,\\
\varepsilon_N&=2h_ya_y\sum_{i=2}^{N-1}\mathsf D_i
+2\beta h_fa_f\mathsf D_N.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:freeze_cost_budget}
\end{equation}
As usual, empty sums equal zero.

\begin{theorem}[Guard-consistent single-freeze optimization gap]
\label{thm:frozen_gap}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:domain}--\ref{ass:reference}, for every $U\in\mathcal F_k$,
\begin{equation}
\left|J_k^F(U)-J_k^{\rm fr}(U;\bar U_k)\right|
\le\varepsilon_N.
\label{eq:objective_discrepancy}
\end{equation}
If $\widehat U_k\in\mathcal F_k$ is primal feasible and the frozen-QP suboptimality obeys
\begin{equation}
0\le J_k^{\rm fr}(\widehat U_k;\bar U_k)
-\min_{U\in\mathcal F_k}J_k^{\rm fr}(U;\bar U_k)
\le\delta_k,
\label{eq:solver_gap}
\end{equation}
then
\begin{equation}
0\le J_k^F(\widehat U_k)-\min_{U\in\mathcal F_k}J_k^F(U)
\le2\varepsilon_N+\delta_k.
\label{eq:frozen_gap}
\end{equation}
The guarantee concerns the \emph{learned complete predictor}, not plant-level optimality.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
At the initialization, the full and frozen coefficient pairs coincide, giving $f_{1|k}=p_{1|k}$. For later steps, inserting $F(p_{i|k},\vu_{i|k})$ and invoking contraction and the uniform frozen-map discrepancy gives $\norm{f_{i+1|k}-p_{i+1|k}}_\Pi\le\lambda\norm{f_{i|k}-p_{i|k}}_\Pi+\bar\eta$. Therefore $\norm{f_{i|k}-p_{i|k}}_\Pi\le\mathsf D_i$. The input-history caches of both predictions are exactly identical under a common input sequence, so every input-increment penalty is the same; the first-input anchor also cancels. For outputs in $\mathcal Q$, the weighted stage and terminal output residuals are bounded by $h_y$ and $h_f$. The difference-of-squares inequality gives~\eqref{eq:objective_discrepancy}. Applying this bound to the returned feasible candidate, then~\eqref{eq:solver_gap}, and then to a complete-model global minimizer \emph{over the same compact set} $\mathcal F_k$ yields~\eqref{eq:frozen_gap}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The stability--performance comparison]
The restriction $\mathcal F_k\subseteq\mathcal U^N$ matters. Relative to the \emph{unguarded} complete-model optimum, the bound becomes $2\varepsilon_N+\delta_k+\mathcal L_k$, where
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_k=\min_{U\in\mathcal F_k}J_k^F(U)
-\min_{U\in\mathcal U^N}J_k^F(U)\ge0.
\label{eq:guard_price}
\end{equation}
Neither common contraction nor coefficient freezing implies that $\mathcal L_k$ is small. This separates the price of freezing from that of imposing a stabilizing guard.
\end{remark}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
{\color{blue}This paper has developed a history-conditioned Transformer--Koopman prediction model with a measurement-preserving lift, recursively updated measurement and input histories, and low-rank transition and input scheduling. A Schur nominal skeleton, together with explicit derivative, residual, and invariant-domain budgets, supplies a constructible class of contractive nonzero-scheduling predictors. The encoder-to-plant consistency requirement remains an independently verifiable assumption rather than an automatic consequence of the model architecture.

For reference tracking, a nominal DC selector transforms prescribed outputs into an internal steady-input candidate. Each sampling instant then requires one complete-model nominal rollout and one strictly convex coefficient-frozen QP. The feasible first-input guard, together with common contraction, guarantees recursive feasibility and practical exponential tracking of the actual re-encoded closed loop. Exponential convergence to zero follows for admissible constant references that are exact equilibria of the complete scheduled model when the re-encoding residual vanishes. In a separate result, the finite-horizon optimization loss caused by freezing is bounded relative to the full learned predictor over the same guard-constrained feasible set.

The conclusions have defined limits: output references must belong to a DC-feasible class, quantitative guarantees depend on model-specific contraction and residual certificates, and the stabilizing guard can reduce predictive-control flexibility. The analytical nonzero-scheduling example demonstrates feasibility of the assumptions, not identification accuracy or optimality for an independently trained Transformer. Learned-model evaluation and empirical controller comparisons are therefore required before asserting predictive or computational superiority.}

\section{Contractive Predictor Construction}
\label{app:construction}
The skeleton in \eqref{eq:lyapunov_skeleton} is block triangular, with eigenvalues those of $\mA_0$ and the nilpotent shift blocks. Thus it is Schur, and $\Pi=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(M_0^T)^jM_0^j$ is positive definite. Its induced norm satisfies $\lambda_0^2=1-1/\lambda_{\max}(\Pi)<1$.

For completeness, the softmax Jacobian $J(\pi)=\operatorname{diag}(\pi)-\pi\pi^T$ has Euclidean norm at most $1/2$. Combining this bound, the $1$-Lipschitz property of $\tanh$, and $\norm{b}_1\le\sqrt r\norm{b}_2$ gives \eqref{eq:Lipschitz_constants}: split the change of $\pi_j\tanh([Wy+b]_j)$ into the activation change and the softmax-weight change, then sum the unit-bounded rank-one factors. The first sum is at most $\norm{W}_2\norm{\Delta y}_2$, the second at most $\sqrt r\norm{W^\pi}_2\norm{\Delta y}_2/2$.

Let $L_c$ bound the context derivative on the selected ball. A two-layer $\tanh$ context has $L_c\le\norm{W_{c,2}}_2\norm{W_{c,1}}_2$. For a single attention head $Y(Z)=P(Z)V(Z)W_O$, $P=\operatorname{softmax}_{\rm row}(Q(Z)K(Z)^T/\sqrt{d_h})$, suppose the affine query, key, and value maps have Frobenius amplitude bounds $M_Q,M_K,M_V$ and Lipschitz constants $L_Q,L_K,L_V$ on a convex token domain. With $L_{\rm tok}$ tokens,
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
L_{\rm score}&\le(L_QM_K+M_QL_K)/\sqrt{d_h},\\
L_Y&\le\norm{W_O}_2\left(\tfrac12M_VL_{\rm score}
+\sqrt{L_{\rm tok}}L_V\right).
\end{split}
\label{eq:attention_bound}
\end{equation}
This follows from the product rule, the rowwise softmax bound, and $\norm{P}_2\le\norm{P}_F\le\sqrt{L_{\rm tok}}$. Multihead concatenation combines the head bounds in Euclidean norm before the final projection. Residual blocks have constant at most one plus the branch constant; feedforward and embedding constants multiply under composition. Layer normalization with fixed positive $\epsilon_{\rm LN}$ has derivative norm at most $\norm{\gamma_{\rm LN}}_\infty/\sqrt{\epsilon_{\rm LN}}$, and mean pooling has norm $1/\sqrt{L_{\rm tok}}$. Fixed positions and biases enter amplitude bounds. These formulas permit constrained Transformer context maps, but do not imply that an unconstrained trained network has a useful small bound. When the temporal encoder uses GELU activations, its feature-map bound additionally incorporates a uniform activation-derivative bound.

Define the norm-conversion factors and domain bounds
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
a_z&=\norm{\Pi^{1/2}E_z}_2,\quad
b_z=\norm{E_z^T\Pi^{-1/2}}_2,\\
b_q&=\norm{\Pi^{-1/2}}_2,\quad
b_x=\norm{C_xE_z^T\Pi^{-1/2}}_2,\\
M_u&=\sup_{u\in\mathcal U}\norm{u}_2,\qquad M_z=b_zM.
\end{split}
\label{eq:norm_conversion}
\end{equation}
For a variation $\Delta q$, with $\Delta\vz=E_z^T\Delta q$, the top block of $D_qF-M_0$ is the sum
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
&(\mA(\vc)-\mA_0)\Delta\vz
+D\mA(\vc)[D\vc(q)\Delta q]\vz\\
&\hspace{14mm}+D\mB(C_x\vz)[C_x\Delta\vz]u.
\end{split}
\label{eq:full_derivative}
\end{equation}
The other blocks vanish. Therefore
\begin{equation}
\nu_{\rm bd}=a_z(g_A b_z+L_A L_cM_zb_q+L_BM_ub_x)
\label{eq:derivative_budget}
\end{equation}
bounds its $\Pi$-norm. The frozen perturbation is bounded by $a_zg_A b_z$, uniformly over all coefficients generated in the ball. Smoothness on the ball and its line segments turns these derivative bounds into same-input Lipschitz bounds.

To give a fully specified gain choice, let

\norm{\Pi^{1/2}\col(\mB_0u,0,E_uu)}_2.
\end{equation*}
Choose a trial ball satisfying~\eqref{eq:radius_margin}. Fix $\bar\kappa_A,\bar\kappa_B,\bar L_c$ so that $\kappa_\chi\le\bar\kappa_\chi$ and $L_c\le\bar L_c$ throughout the allowed parameter set on this ball. Take $g_A=t\widehat g_A$, $g_B=t\widehat g_B$ with $\widehat g_A,\widehat g_B>0$, and set

\begin{split}
k_F&=a_z(\widehat g_A b_z+\widehat g_A\bar\kappa_A\bar L_c b_zMb_q
+\widehat g_B\bar\kappa_BM_ub_x),\\
k_{\rm fr}&=a_z\widehat g_A b_z,\quad k_b=a_z\widehat g_BM_u,
\quad k_* =\max(k_F,k_{\rm fr}).
\end{split}
\label{eq:gain_coefficients}
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
0<t<\min\left\{1,\frac{1-\lambda_0}{2k_*},
\frac{\Delta}{2(k_*M+k_b)}\right\}
\label{eq:gain_choice}
\end{equation}
works, omitting zero-denominator terms. The ball is chosen before the gains; a residual bound available only on a smaller set therefore cannot justify enlarging it. A nontrivial member of the mathematical class takes $n=m=1$, $P_h=2$, $\vz=(x,\psi)$, $\psi(X)=\alpha_{\rm d} x_{\rm past}$, and $\alpha_{\rm d}\ne0$. Set $c=a+v$ and

\mB(x)=\begin{bmatrix}b_0+g_B\tanh x\\0\end{bmatrix},
\label{eq:analytic_example}
\end{equation}
where $|a_0|<1$, $b_0\ne0$, and both positive gains obey \eqref{eq:gain_choice}. These are rank-one corrections with genuine history and measurement dependence. Since $\psi^+=\alpha_{\rm d}x=\alpha_{\rm d}a^+$, the encoding graph is preserved. On an input box containing zero in its interior, a small alternating input $\vu_t^r=h(-1)^t$ has a unique periodic state in the already invariant ball: its two-step map is a contraction. At $h=0$, its linearization gives $x_t^r=-b_0h(-1)^t/(1+a_0)+o(h)$, so the measurement is nonconstant for sufficiently small nonzero $h$. This delay-coordinate example establishes nonemptiness of the model class, not exact representation or encoding consistency of an arbitrary Transformer.

Finally, the actual residual interface can be decomposed without assuming invariance of the encoder image. At an encoded initial point, let $\widehat\vz$ be the predicted next lift, $\widehat x=C_x\widehat\vz$, and $\widetilde X=\operatorname{shift}(\mX,\widehat x)$. If $C_\psi$ selects the learned feature block and $E_X$ appends a window-end perturbation, define
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\nu&=x^+-\widehat x,\qquad
\kappa=\psi_\theta(\widetilde X)-C_\psi\widehat\vz,\\
r&=\col\bigl(\nu,\kappa+
\psi_\theta(\widetilde X+E_X\nu)-\psi_\theta(\widetilde X)\bigr).
\end{split}
\label{eq:encoded_residual_decomposition}
\end{equation}
With exact caches, $w=\col(r,0,0)$. Uniform bounds $\bar\nu,\bar\kappa$ and a feature Lipschitz constant $L_\psi$ on all these windows give the sufficient budget $\bar w=a_z\sqrt{\bar\nu^2+(\bar\kappa+L_\psi\bar\nu)^2}$. The two terms measure physical measurement-prediction error and encoding-graph defect, respectively; neither is supplied by the gain construction alone.

\section{Complete-versus-Frozen Prediction Estimates}

For a fixed candidate $U$, let $f_i,p_i$ be its full and frozen trajectories. Both have identical initial conditions and the same first-step coefficient evaluation. By Assumption~\ref{ass:domain}, both remain inside $\mathcal Q$. For $i\ge1$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\norm{f_{i+1}-p_{i+1}}_\Pi
&\le\norm{F(f_i,\vu_i)-F(p_i,\vu_i)}_\Pi\\
&\quad+\norm{F(p_i,\vu_i)-F_{\bar q_i}^{\rm fr}(p_i,\vu_i)}_\Pi\\
&\le\lambda\norm{f_i-p_i}_\Pi+\bar\eta.
\end{split}
\label{eq:full_frozen_recursion}
\end{equation}
Because $f_0=p_0$ and $f_1=p_1$, the resulting bound is exactly $\mathsf D_i$ from~\eqref{eq:frozen_discrepancy}. The uniform discrepancy may be conservatively bounded using the modulation amplitudes. In the original low-rank class, for $q,\bar q\in\mathcal Q$,
\begin{equation}
\bar\eta\le2\norm{\Pi^{1/2}E_z}_2\bigl(
 g_A\norm{E_z^T\Pi^{-1/2}}_2M+g_B\bar u\bigr).
\label{eq:freeze_amplitude_budget}
\end{equation}
The factor two allows both coefficient evaluations to vary over their full admissible ranges; a tighter schedule-specific bound may be substituted.

To verify the output-cost budget, note that for any $q\in\mathcal Q$ and $\norm{\vr}\le\bar r$,
\begin{equation}
\norm{Cq-\vr}_{Q_y}\le h_y,\qquad
\norm{Cq-\vr}_{Q_f}\le h_f.
\end{equation}
For two states $q,q'\in\mathcal Q$ at the same absolute time, the difference of squared output costs satisfies
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\big|\norm{Cq-\vr}_{Q_y}^2-\norm{Cq'-\vr}_{Q_y}^2\big|
&\le2h_ya_y\norm{q-q'}_\Pi,
\end{split}
\label{eq:output_cost_continuity}
\end{equation}
with the analogous terminal bound $2h_fa_f\norm{q-q'}_\Pi$. Crucially, the previous-input cache propagates as the same applied candidate input in both trajectories; therefore the entire vector of increments $\vu_i-E_{\rm prev}q_i$ is identical, even if the lifted state and measurement cache differ. The anchor depends only on $\vu_0$ and $\vu_k^b$ and cancels as well. Summing only the remaining output-cost differences yields~\eqref{eq:freeze_cost_budget}; the two applications of this uniform comparison establish Theorem~\ref{thm:frozen_gap}.

A solver need not be exactly optimal for the tracking theorem, but a numerical study quoting the optimization gap~\eqref{eq:frozen_gap} should certify the returned primal feasible sequence and an objective gap $\delta_k$, e.g., via a primal-dual lower bound for the same constrained QP. A violation of the first-input guard, even by a solver tolerance, is outside the exact statement of Theorem~\ref{thm:tracking} unless its effect is explicitly added to the error budget.

The numerical constants are evaluations of these global analytic inequalities, not maxima sampled along the trajectory.

At the selected nominal equilibrium, $x_1=x_\star$ and $c(q^b)=0$, so both scheduled corrections vanish \emph{at that point} and $F(q^b,\vu^b)=q^b$. They remain genuinely nonzero on transients. Thus with $\bar d_b=\bar\nu_b=\bar w=0$, $\kappa=.08$, and~\eqref{eq:analytic_scheduled_rates}, one obtains $\rho_g\le0.9515304<1$. Theorem~\ref{thm:tracking} consequently proves \emph{exact exponential output tracking} for this nonlinear analytical example. This calculation demonstrates nonemptiness of the sufficient-condition class; it does not certify the weights of a separately trained Transformer.
