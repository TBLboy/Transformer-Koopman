\title{Transformer--Koopman: History-Conditioned Scheduled Prediction and Frozen-LTV Model Predictive Control}
\author{Bolin Tao}
\markboth{IEEE Journal Draft}{Tao: Transformer--Koopman}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Koopman-inspired predictors provide a structured representation of nonlinear dynamics by evolving observables through finite-dimensional linear maps. A fixed lifted transition, however, can be restrictive when the measured state does not determine the relevant operating condition. This paper develops a history-conditioned scheduled predictor in which a temporal lifting map is complemented by a context-dependent low-rank transition and a measurement-dependent input modulation. The transition and input matrices are bounded functions of a history context and the current lifted measurement, respectively. For fixed scheduling signals the predictor is linear in the lifted state and input, while recursive scheduling remains generally nonlinear. Under common-history, work-domain, Lipschitz, and one-step residual conditions, we derive a finite-horizon open-loop prediction bound. For control, a nominal rollout supplies scheduling matrices that are frozen over the horizon, yielding an LTV prediction model and a convex quadratic program under polyhedral input constraints. We further derive a finite-horizon bound between the complete scheduled model and its frozen approximation, and state a conditional cost-suboptimality result relative to the global optimum of the complete problem.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
Koopman modeling, temporal lifting, scheduled prediction, low-rank modulation, LTV-MPC, model predictive control.
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
% =========================================================

Accurate multi-step prediction is central to model-based control of nonlinear dynamical systems. In many applications, however, the available measurements do not uniquely determine the underlying physical state. Similar measurements can therefore be followed by different responses to the same control input, depending on the preceding evolution of the system. Using a history of measurements and inputs can help distinguish these situations. For model predictive control (MPC), the resulting predictor must also support repeated rollouts and optimization over a finite horizon \cite{ljung1999system,nelles2020nonlinear,forgione2021dynonet}.

Koopman-based modeling offers a way to combine nonlinear system representations with structured prediction. The Koopman operator describes the evolution of functions of the system state, known as observables, through a linear action, even when the underlying dynamics are nonlinear \cite{koopman1931hamiltonian,mezic2005spectral,brunton2022modern}. Because this representation is generally infinite-dimensional, practical methods approximate it in a finite set of lifted coordinates. Examples include extended dynamic mode decomposition and lifted predictors incorporating control inputs \cite{williams2015data,proctor2016dmdc,korda2018linear}. Neural Koopman approaches further learn the lifted coordinates from data \cite{takeishi2017koopman,lusch2018deep,otto2019linearly,han2020deep}. Their usefulness for multi-step prediction depends on how well the lifted coordinates capture the relevant dynamics and how accurately the finite-dimensional evolution model propagates them.

One line of research incorporates temporal information into the lifted representation. Delay-coordinate methods use past measurements to construct observables \cite{kamb2020timedelay,das2019delay}, while recurrent encoders and Transformer-based models learn features from observation histories \cite{han2024robust,shenoy2025temporal,forootani2025deepkoopformer}. A complementary line adapts the evolution model to the observed context. Koopa employs context-dependent operators for time-series forecasting \cite{liu2023koopa}, and MamKO generates Koopman matrices from recent data for prediction and control \cite{li2025mamko}. These developments make it possible to use history both to represent the system and to adjust its predicted dynamics. In a controlled recursive rollout, however, future histories contain predicted measurements and candidate inputs. The evolution matrices can then change with the trajectory being predicted. This coupling affects the propagation of prediction errors and makes direct MPC optimization with the fully scheduled model generally nonlinear. The central problem considered here is how to retain history-dependent dynamics while obtaining an analyzable multi-step predictor and a tractable MPC formulation.

We address this problem with a history-conditioned, Koopman-inspired predictor and a frozen linear time-varying (LTV) MPC formulation. A temporal Transformer extracts lifted features from a finite measurement history, while the current measurement is retained explicitly in the lifted state. A separate network uses past measurements and inputs to schedule the lifted transition matrix; the input matrix is modulated by the current measured coordinates. Both matrices are expressed as nominal matrices with bounded low-rank corrections. We analyze recursive prediction with histories updated from the model outputs and derive a finite-horizon error bound that accounts for history-dependent scheduling. For control, scheduling matrices are evaluated along a nominal rollout and held fixed during each optimization. This produces an LTV prediction model whose quadratic tracking objective and polyhedral input constraints lead to a convex quadratic program. We also bound the discrepancy introduced by freezing the matrices and derive a conditional bound on the resulting control-cost suboptimality.

The main contributions are as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \emph{History-conditioned lifted prediction:} We construct a measurement-preserving temporal lifting and a scheduled predictor with bounded low-rank modulation of the transition and input matrices.
    \item \emph{Finite-horizon prediction analysis:} We define a recursive rollout with updated measurement and input histories and derive an error bound that incorporates the effect of prediction errors on subsequent matrix scheduling.
    \item \emph{Frozen-LTV predictive control:} We formulate a nominal-trajectory-based convex QP for MPC and establish bounds on the frozen-schedule prediction discrepancy and conditional control-cost suboptimality.
\end{enumerate}

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section~\ref{sec:prelim} states the prediction and control problems. Section~\ref{sec:model} presents the predictor and its error analysis. Section~\ref{sec:control} develops frozen-LTV MPC and the associated approximation bounds. Sections~\ref{sec:experiments} and~\ref{sec:conclusion} present the experimental protocol and conclusions, respectively. Fig.~\ref{fig:overview} shows the overall framework.

\section{Preliminaries and Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:prelim}
% =========================================================

\subsection{System Description and Koopman Background}

Consider the discrete-time nonlinear controlled system
\begin{equation}
    \vs_{k+1}=f(\vs_k,\vu_k),\qquad \vx_k=h(\vs_k),
    \label{eq:physical_system}
\end{equation}
where $\vs_k\in\mathcal S\subseteq\R^{n_s}$ is the underlying state, $f:\mathcal S\times\R^m\to\mathcal S$ is the state transition, $\vu_k\in\R^m$ is the control input, and $\vx_k=h(\vs_k)\in\R^n$ is the measured output with $h:\mathcal S\to\R^n$. The predictor is constructed from measurements and applied inputs; the underlying state serves only to state the physical system.

For a fixed input $\vu$, the controlled Koopman operator maps an observable $\varphi:\mathcal S\to\R$ according to
\begin{equation}
    (\mathcal K_{\vu}\varphi)(\vs)=\varphi(f(\vs,\vu)).
    \label{eq:koopman_operator}
\end{equation}
This operator is linear in the observable, although the state dynamics can be nonlinear. Finite-dimensional Koopman models replace the infinite observable space by selected coordinates with a structured evolution law. Here the coordinates are constructed from a finite measurement history rather than from an assumed instantaneous state observable. The resulting matrices therefore define a history-based controlled prediction parameterization in the selected lifted coordinates.

Finite-dimensional approximations select observables and model their evolution with matrices in the resulting coordinates. With an instantaneous measurement lifting, this construction has the familiar form
\begin{equation}
    \vz_k=g(\vx_k)\in\R^d,
    \qquad
    \vz_{k+1}\approx\mA\vz_k+\mB\vu_k.
    \label{eq:instantaneous_lifting}
\end{equation}
The transition $\mA\in\R^{d\times d}$ describes the selected lifted coordinates, and $\mB\in\R^{d\times m}$ represents the contribution of the control input. When an instantaneous measurement does not distinguish the relevant dynamical condition, a finite observation history can instead supply the lifting coordinates. This motivates the history-based construction below.

For a vector $a$, let $\norm{a}_2$ denote the Euclidean norm; for a matrix, $\norm{M}_2$ denotes the spectral norm. For a positive semidefinite matrix $M$, write $\norm{a}_M^2=a^\top M a$.

\subsection{History-Based Prediction and Control Problem}

Let the available data consist of measured input--output trajectories
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal D=\{\tau^{(j)}\}_{j=1}^{N_{\mathrm{tr}}},
 \qquad
 \tau^{(j)}=\{(\vx_t^{(j)},\vu_t^{(j)})\}_{t=0}^{T_j}.
 \label{eq:trajectory_dataset}
\end{equation}
For each admissible physical time $k$, let $P\ge2$ and assume that the required prehistory is available. The measurement and past-input histories are
\begin{align}
    \mX_k&=\begin{bmatrix}\vx_{k-P+1}\\ \vdots\\ \vx_k\end{bmatrix}\in\R^{Pn},
    \label{eq:measurement_window}\\
    \mU_k^{\mathrm{in}}&=\begin{bmatrix}\vu_{k-P+1}\\ \vdots\\ \vu_{k-1}\end{bmatrix}\in\R^{(P-1)m}.
    \label{eq:history_windows}
\end{align}
The history contains $P$ measurements and $P-1$ previously applied inputs; the current input $\vu_k$ follows the observed history and enters the next-state update separately. Given $(\mX_k,\mU_k^{\mathrm{in}})$ and a prescribed sequence $\vu_{k:k+H-1}$, the prediction problem is to produce $H$ future measurement estimates $(\hat\vx_{k+1|k},\ldots,\hat\vx_{k+H|k})$. The predictor must use the observed history to represent the current dynamical condition and then remain consistent under recursive multi-step propagation.

For control, the input sequence $U=(\vu_0,\ldots,\vu_{N-1})$ is selected over a horizon $N$ to track measurement references subject to admissible input constraints. The corresponding scheduled prediction model and the frozen-LTV control construction are developed in Sections~\ref{sec:model} and~\ref{sec:control}, respectively.

We use $k$ for physical time and $i\ge0$ for the prediction step relative to time $k$. Thus $i=0$ denotes the initial history and the relative input $\vu_i$ corresponds to the physical-time input $\vu_{k+i}$. The prediction horizon $H$, the training rollout horizon $H_R$, and the control horizon $N$ serve distinct purposes.

The measurement history supplies the lifting map, whereas the past-input history is available to the history context introduced in Section~\ref{sec:model}. This separation allows the lifted state to represent temporal measurements while the scheduled dynamics use the recent input--output context.

The predictor lifts the measurement history while retaining the current measurement as an explicit coordinate:
\begin{align}
    \vz_k&=g_\theta(\mX_k)=\begin{bmatrix}\vx_k\\ \psi_\theta(\mX_k)\end{bmatrix}\in\R^d,
    \label{eq:state_preserving_lifting}\\
    \vx_k&=C_x\vz_k,\qquad d=n+n_\psi,
    \label{eq:state_readout}
\end{align}
where $\psi_\theta:\R^{Pn}\to\R^{n_\psi}$ and $C_x=[I_n\;0]\in\R^{n\times d}$. At an observed physical time, $\vz_k^{\mathrm{enc}}=g_\theta(\mX_k)$ satisfies $C_x\vz_k^{\mathrm{enc}}=\vx_k$. During recursive prediction, the propagated state starts at $\vz_0^{\mathrm{prop}}=\vz_k^{\mathrm{enc}}$ and its readout $C_x\vz_i^{\mathrm{prop}}$ is the predicted measurement at step $i$.

All prediction and control comparisons use the same initial measurement and past-input histories and the same encoded initial state. Before the rollout starts, history slots contain recorded measurements; after each step, the oldest measurement and input slots are replaced by $C_x\vz_{i+1}^{\mathrm{prop}}$ and $\vu_i$, respectively. This convention supplies the common initial condition for the prediction and control analyses. Fig.~\ref{fig:rollout} illustrates the update.

\section{Scheduled Koopman Modeling}
\label{sec:model}
% =========================================================

\subsection{Model construction}

The lifting map uses a patch-based temporal Transformer encoder \cite{vaswani2017attention,nie2023time}. Let $p\in\mathbb N_{\ge1}$ be the patch length, with $p\le P$ and $p\mid P$. Partition $\mX_k$ into $N_p=P/p$ contiguous non-overlapping patches and vectorize each patch as $\vq_k^{(j)}\in\R^{pn}$. A shared linear embedding maps $\vq_k^{(j)}$ to a token in $\R^{d_{\mathrm{model}}}$; sinusoidal positional encoding is then added before the Transformer encoder. Mean pooling of the $N_p$ contextualized tokens, followed by a linear projection to $\R^{n_\psi}$, gives $\psi_\theta(\mX_k)$. Concatenating this history feature with $\vx_k$ produces the measurement-preserving lift in \eqref{eq:state_preserving_lifting}. A separate feed-forward map $C_\phi$ processes the flattened measurement and past-input windows.

The history context is
\begin{align}
    \vc_k&=C_\phi(\mX_k,\mU_k^{\mathrm{in}})\in\R^{d_c},
    \label{eq:history_context}\\
    \vchi_k&=C_x\vz_k\in\R^n.
    \label{eq:scheduling_variables}
\end{align}
The scheduled predictor is
\begin{equation}
    \vz_{k+1}=\mA(\vc_k)\vz_k+\mB(\vchi_k)\vu_k.
    \label{eq:scheduled_predictor}
\end{equation}
The map $\mA$ is scheduled by history, while $\mB$ is scheduled by the current measured component of the lifted state.

We use bounded low-rank corrections around nominal matrices,
\begin{align}
    \mA(\vc)&=\mA_0+\mU_A\operatorname{diag}(\gamma_A(\vc))\mV_A^\top,
    \label{eq:transition_schedule}\\
    \mB(\vchi)&=\mB_0+\mU_B\operatorname{diag}(\gamma_B(\vchi))\mV_B^\top.
    \label{eq:low_rank_schedules}
\end{align}
Here $r_A,r_B\in\mathbb N_{\ge1}$, $g_A,g_B\ge0$, $\mA_0\in\R^{d\times d}$, $\mB_0\in\R^{d\times m}$, $\mU_A,\mV_A\in\R^{d\times r_A}$, $\mU_B\in\R^{d\times r_B}$, and $\mV_B\in\R^{m\times r_B}$. The vectors $u_{A,j}$, $v_{A,j}$, $u_{B,j}$, and $v_{B,j}$ below denote the corresponding factor columns. The transition coefficients have the form
\begin{align}
    \gamma_{A,j}(\vc)&=g_A\pi_{A,j}(\vc)\tanh([W_A\vc+b_A]_j),
    \label{eq:A_modulation_coefficients}\\
    \pi_A(\vc)&=\operatorname{softmax}(W_A^\pi\vc+b_A^\pi),
    \label{eq:A_modulation}
\end{align}
where $W_A,W_A^\pi\in\R^{r_A\times d_c}$ and $b_A,b_A^\pi\in\R^{r_A}$. The input-modulation coefficients are defined analogously by
\begin{align}
    \gamma_{B,j}(\vchi)&=g_B\pi_{B,j}(\vchi)\tanh([W_B\vchi+b_B]_j),\\
    \pi_B(\vchi)&=\operatorname{softmax}(W_B^\pi\vchi+b_B^\pi),
\end{align}
where $W_B,W_B^\pi\in\R^{r_B\times n}$ and $b_B,b_B^\pi\in\R^{r_B}$. Throughout this subsection, the column-normalization condition
\begin{equation}
 \norm{u_{A,j}}_2,\norm{v_{A,j}}_2,\norm{u_{B,j}}_2,\norm{v_{B,j}}_2\le1
 \label{eq:factor_norm_assumption}
\end{equation}
is imposed on the factor parameterization. Together with the prescribed nonnegative gains, it bounds the correction amplitudes.

\begin{proposition}[Uniform modulation bounds]
\label{prop:modulation_bound}
For every $\vc\in\R^{d_c}$ and $\vchi\in\R^n$,
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    \norm{\mA(\vc)-\mA_0}_2&\le g_A,\\
    \norm{\mB(\vchi)-\mB_0}_2&\le g_B.
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:modulation_bound}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Writing the first correction as $\sum_{j=1}^{r_A}\gamma_{A,j}u_{A,j}v_{A,j}^{\top}$ and using $\norm{uv^{\top}}_2=\norm u_2\norm v_2$ gives $\norm{\mA(\vc)-\mA_0}_2\le\sum_{j=1}^{r_A}|\gamma_{A,j}(\vc)|$. Since $g_A\ge0$, softmax weights are nonnegative and sum to one, and $|\tanh(\cdot)|<1$, $|\gamma_{A,j}(\vc)|\le g_A\pi_{A,j}(\vc)$, which proves the first bound. The argument for $\mB$ is identical.
\end{proof}

The schedule maps are also Lipschitz under finite modulation-layer weights. Define
\begin{align}
    L_A&=g_A\left(\norm{W_A}_2+\frac{\sqrt{r_A}}{2}\norm{W_A^\pi}_2\right),
    \label{eq:transition_Lipschitz_constant}\\
    L_B&=g_B\left(\norm{W_B}_2+\frac{\sqrt{r_B}}{2}\norm{W_B^\pi}_2\right).
    \label{eq:Lipschitz_constants}
\end{align}
Then
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    \norm{\mA(\vc)-\mA(\vc')}_2&\le L_A\norm{\vc-\vc'}_2,\\
    \norm{\mB(\vchi)-\mB(\vchi')}_2&\le L_B\norm{\vchi-\vchi'}_2.
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:schedule_lipschitz}
\end{equation}
The factor $1/2$ is a global Euclidean Lipschitz constant of softmax.

\subsection{Two-Stage Training and Recursive Prediction}

Each training segment contains a measured history $(\mX_k,\mU_k^{\mathrm{in}})$, a rollout horizon $H_R$, the future inputs $(\vu_k,\ldots,\vu_{k+H_R-1})$, and the corresponding measurements $(\vx_{k+1},\ldots,\vx_{k+H_R})$. The training horizon $H_R$ is distinct from the prediction-analysis horizon $H$ and the control horizon $N$.

\textit{Stage 0: nominal one-step pretraining.} Let $\mathcal I_1$ be the set of available one-step transitions. The encoder and nominal dynamics are initialized by
\begin{align}
 \vz_k^{E}&=g_\theta(\mX_k),\\
 \hat\vz_{k+1|k}^{P,0}&=\mA_0\vz_k^{E}+\mB_0\vu_k,\\
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm{init}}
 &=\frac{1}{|\mathcal I_1|}\sum_{k\in\mathcal I_1}
 \norm{C_x\hat\vz_{k+1|k}^{P,0}-\vx_{k+1}}_2^2.
 \label{eq:training_one_step_objects}
\end{align}
Stage 0 minimizes $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{init}}$ with respect to $\theta$, $\mA_0$, and $\mB_0$. Because $C_x=[I_n\;0]$, this objective directly supervises the measured coordinates of the one-step prediction.

\textit{Stage 1: scheduled rollout training.} The scheduled maps in \eqref{eq:transition_schedule}--\eqref{eq:low_rank_schedules} are activated and the complete parameterization is jointly refined. For each segment, initialize
\begin{equation}
 \vz_{k|k}^{P}=g_\theta(\mX_k),\qquad
 \hat{\mX}_{k|k}=\mX_k,\qquad
 \hat{\mU}_{k|k}^{\mathrm{in}}=\mU_k^{\mathrm{in}}.
 \label{eq:training_rollout_initialization}
\end{equation}
For $i=0,\ldots,H_R-1$, propagate and update the histories by
\begin{align}
 \vc_{k+i|k}^{P}&=C_\phi(\hat{\mX}_{k+i|k},
 \hat{\mU}_{k+i|k}^{\mathrm{in}}),\\
 \vchi_{k+i|k}^{P}&=C_x\vz_{k+i|k}^{P},\\
 \vz_{k+i+1|k}^{P}&=\mA(\vc_{k+i|k}^{P})\vz_{k+i|k}^{P}
  +\mB(\vchi_{k+i|k}^{P})\vu_{k+i},
  \label{eq:recursive_prediction}\\
 \hat\vx_{k+i+1|k}&=C_x\vz_{k+i+1|k}^{P}.
 \label{eq:training_rollout_update}
\end{align}
The histories are shifted as $\hat{\mX}_{k+i+1|k}=\operatorname{shift}(\hat{\mX}_{k+i|k},\hat\vx_{k+i+1|k})$ and $\hat{\mU}_{k+i+1|k}^{\mathrm{in}}=\operatorname{shift}(\hat{\mU}_{k+i|k}^{\mathrm{in}},\vu_{k+i})$. Thus the encoder initializes a rollout once; every later context and input matrix is evaluated from the propagated state and shifted predicted history.

Let $\operatorname{sg}[\cdot]$ denote stop-gradient and define the encoded target $\vz_{k+i}^{E}=g_\theta(\mX_{k+i})$. Stage 1 minimizes the single joint rollout objective
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
  \mathcal L_{\mathrm{joint}}
  ={}&\frac{1}{|\mathcal I_R|H_R}
  \sum_{k\in\mathcal I_R}\sum_{i=1}^{H_R}
  \bigl[\lambda_x\norm{\hat\vx_{k+i|k}-\vx_{k+i}}_2^2\\
  &\quad+\lambda_z\norm{\vz_{k+i|k}^{P}
  -\operatorname{sg}[\vz_{k+i}^{E}]}_2^2\bigr],\\
  &\lambda_x,\lambda_z>0,
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:joint_training_loss}
\end{equation}
where $\lambda_x$ and $\lambda_z$ are fixed training weights. The first term enforces multi-step measurement prediction, and the second term constrains propagated lifted states against re-encodings of the measured histories. This same propagated recurrence is used at deployment without the supervised targets.

\subsection{Finite-horizon prediction analysis}

Let $\vz_{k+i}^{\mathrm{enc}}$ be the encoder sequence formed from the true measurement windows and let $\hat\vz_i$ be the propagated sequence under the same input sequence. Define
\begin{equation}
    \ve_i=\vz_{k+i}^{\mathrm{enc}}-\hat\vz_i,
    \qquad \ve_0=0.
    \label{eq:prediction_error}
\end{equation}
The corresponding one-step latent residual is
\begin{equation}
    \vr_{k}=\vz_{k+1}^{\mathrm{enc}}-\mA(\vc_k)\vz_k^{\mathrm{enc}}-\mB(\vchi_k)\vu_k
    \label{eq:residual_definition}
\end{equation}
The following assumptions are local to one fixed finite-horizon comparison.
\begin{assumption}[A1: common finite work domain]
\label{ass:a1}
For fixed initial measured histories, parameters, horizon $H$, and future input sequence, there are compact sets $\mathcal Z\subset\R^d$, $\mathcal C\subset\R^{d_c}$, and $\mathcal U\subset\R^m$, and a joint-window domain $\mathcal D_{\rm win}\subset\R^{Pn+(P-1)m}$, such that the truth-encoded and propagated trajectories remain in $\mathcal Z$, their context outputs remain in $\mathcal C$, and their joint windows remain in $\mathcal D_{\rm win}$. Let $M_Z=\sup_{z\in\mathcal Z}\norm{z}_2$ and $M_U=\sup_{u\in\mathcal U}\norm{u}_2$.
\end{assumption}
\begin{assumption}[A2: common-window context regularity]
\label{ass:a2}
On $\mathcal D_{\rm win}$, $C_\phi$ is Lipschitz with one constant $L_\phi$ for every pair of joint windows:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&\norm{C_\phi(W,V)-C_\phi(W',V')}_2\\
&\quad\le L_\phi\norm{(W,V)-(W',V')}_2.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
\end{assumption}
\begin{assumption}[A3: uniform one-step residual]
\label{ass:a3}
For all encoded transitions $k+i$ with $i=0,\ldots,H-1$ in the common work domain, the residual in \eqref{eq:residual_definition} satisfies $\norm{\vr_{k+i}}_2\le\delta$ for one common $\delta\ge0$.
\end{assumption}
For this comparison, define
\begin{align}
    M_A&=\norm{\mA_0}_2+g_A,
    \label{eq:transition_norm_bound}\\
    L&=M_A+L_BM_U+L_AL_\phi\sqrt{P}M_Z.
    \label{eq:prediction_L}
\end{align}
The joint training objective motivates the residual comparison, while Assumption~\ref{ass:a3} supplies the uniform finite-horizon bound required below.

\begin{proposition}[One-step prediction recurrence]
\label{prop:prediction_recurrence}
For every $0\le i\le H-1$, define $E_i=\max_{\max\{0,i-P+1\}\le j\le i}\norm{\ve_j}_2$. Then
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\norm{\ve_{i+1}}_2\le{}& \delta+(M_A+L_BM_U)\norm{\ve_i}_2\\
 &+L_AL_\phi\sqrt{P}M_Z
  E_i,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:prediction_recurrence}
\end{equation}
where $\norm{C_x}_2=1$ under $C_x=[I_n\;0]$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Subtract the propagated update from the encoder update and add and subtract the encoder-scheduled terms:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\ve_{i+1}={}&\vr_{k+i}\\
&+\left[\mA(\vc_{k+i}^{\mathrm{enc}})\vz_{k+i}^{\mathrm{enc}}-\mA(\vc_i^{\mathrm{prop}})\hat\vz_i\right]\\
&+\left[\mB(\vchi_{k+i}^{\mathrm{enc}})-\mB(\vchi_i^{\mathrm{prop}})\right]\vu_{k+i}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The first term is bounded by Assumption~\ref{ass:a3}. For the transition branch, use
\begin{align*}
 &\mA(\vc_{k+i}^{\mathrm{enc}})\vz_{k+i}^{\mathrm{enc}}-
 \mA(\vc_i^{\mathrm{prop}})\hat\vz_i\\
 &=\mA(\vc_{k+i}^{\mathrm{enc}})\ve_i+
 [\mA(\vc_{k+i}^{\mathrm{enc}})-\mA(\vc_i^{\mathrm{prop}})]\hat\vz_i.
\end{align*}
The first term is bounded by $M_A\norm{\ve_i}_2$, and the second by $L_A M_Z$ times the context difference. The input portions of the two windows are identical, while each predicted measurement slot differs by $C_x\ve_j$ and each pre-horizon slot is shared. Thus the joint-window difference is at most $\norm{C_x}_2\sqrt P E_i$; under $C_x=[I_n\;0]$, $\norm{C_x}_2=1$, and Assumption~\ref{ass:a2} gives the displayed context term. For the input branch, \eqref{eq:schedule_lipschitz} gives $L_B\norm{C_x\ve_i}_2\norm{\vu_{k+i}}_2\le L_BM_U\norm{\ve_i}_2$. Combining the three bounds proves \eqref{eq:prediction_recurrence}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite-horizon open-loop prediction bound]
\label{thm:prediction_bound}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:a1}--\ref{ass:a3} and the structural conditions above, define $W_i=\max_{0\le j\le i}\norm{\ve_j}_2$, so that $W_0=0$. Then, for every $0\le i\le H$,
\begin{align}
    \norm{\ve_i}_2&\le W_i\le \delta\sum_{t=0}^{i-1}L^t,
    \label{eq:prediction_bound}
\end{align}
Thus, for $L\ne1$, $\norm{\ve_H}_2\le\delta(L^H-1)/(L-1)$, and for $L=1$, $\norm{\ve_H}_2\le H\delta$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The residual, state-propagation, schedule-difference, and measurement-dependent input terms give \eqref{eq:prediction_recurrence}. Since $E_i\le W_i$, one obtains $\norm{\ve_{i+1}}_2\le\delta+LW_i$. Let $S_0=0$ and $S_{i+1}=\delta+LS_i$. The sequence $S_i=\delta\sum_{t=0}^{i-1}L^t$ is nondecreasing for $L\ge0$, since $S_{i+1}-S_i=\delta L^i$. Induction in $W_{i+1}=\max(W_i,\norm{\ve_{i+1}}_2)$ yields $W_i\le S_i$.
\end{proof}

The bound is open-loop and finite-horizon; it does not require $L<1$, although it can grow with the horizon. The corresponding measurement-space error satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \norm{\vx_{k+i}-C_x\hat\vz_i}_2
 =\norm{C_x\ve_i}_2\le\norm{C_x}_2\norm{\ve_i}_2.
 \label{eq:measurement_prediction_bound}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Complete scheduled problem and freezing construction}

At a control instant, let $\vz_0=\vz_k^{\mathrm{enc}}$ and let $U=(\vu_0,\ldots,\vu_{N-1})$. Assume the input set $\mathcal U\subset\R^m$ is nonempty, compact, and convex; in the standard QP case it is a box or another polytope. For the input-only formulation, define the common feasible set $\mathcal U_{\mathrm{feas}}=\mathcal U^N$. The complete scheduled model uses
\begin{equation}
    \vz_{i+1}=\mA(\vc_i)\vz_i+\mB(C_x\vz_i)\vu_i,
    \label{eq:complete_control_model}
\end{equation}
with history slots shifted according to Section~\ref{sec:prelim}. Given measurement references $\vx_i^{\mathrm{ref}}$, input references $\vu_i^{\mathrm{ref}}$, previous input $\vu_{-1}$, and positive semidefinite weights $Q_x,P_x,R,S$, define
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
J_{\mathrm{full}}(U)= {}&\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\norm{C_x\vz_i-\vx_i^{\mathrm{ref}}}_{Q_x}^2\\
 &+\norm{C_x\vz_N-\vx_N^{\mathrm{ref}}}_{P_x}^2\\
 &+\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\norm{\vu_i-\vu_i^{\mathrm{ref}}}_{R}^2\\
 &+\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\norm{\vu_i-\vu_{i-1}}_{S}^2.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:full_cost}
\end{equation}
The tracking cost is expressed in measurement space; no reference is assigned to the hidden lifted coordinates. The complete problem is generally nonlinear and nonconvex because the scheduling variables depend on the decision-dependent trajectory.

Choose a feasible nominal input sequence $\bar U=(\bar\vu_0,\ldots,\bar\vu_{N-1})$ and initialize the nominal rollout with the same state and pre-horizon history, $\bar\vz_0=\vz_0$, $\bar\mX_0=\mX_k$, and $\bar U^{\mathrm{in}}_0=\mU_k^{\mathrm{in}}$. At each step set $\bar\vchi_i=C_x\bar\vz_i$ and $\bar\vc_i=C_\phi(\bar\mX_i,\bar U_i^{\mathrm{in}})$, then roll out the complete model once:
\begin{equation}
    \bar\vz_{i+1}=\mA(\bar\vc_i)\bar\vz_i+\mB(\bar\vchi_i)\bar\vu_i.
    \label{eq:nominal_rollout}
\end{equation}
Set
\begin{align}
    \bar\mA_i&=\mA(\bar\vc_i),
    \label{eq:frozen_transition_matrix}\\
    \bar\mB_i&=\mB(\bar\vchi_i),
    \label{eq:frozen_matrices}
\end{align}
and keep these matrices fixed while optimizing the candidate sequence $U$. The frozen trajectory is therefore
\begin{equation}
    \vz_{i+1}^{\mathrm{fr}}=\bar\mA_i\vz_i^{\mathrm{fr}}+\bar\mB_i\vu_i.
    \label{eq:frozen_model}
\end{equation}
The nominal rollout, complete trajectory, and frozen trajectory are distinct objects: they use different input sequences and/or different scheduling rules. Fig.~\ref{fig:freezing} illustrates the construction.

\begin{assumption}[A4: uniform control comparison domain]
\label{ass:a4}
Fix $\bar U\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{feas}}$. For every candidate $U\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{feas}}$, the full, frozen, and nominal trajectories remain in shared compact lifted-state and context-output domains; all joint windows remain in $\mathcal D_{\rm win}$; and the same constants $M_Z$, $M_U$, and $L_\phi$ apply. The input-window domain $\mathcal D_{\rm win}$ and the context-output domain $\mathcal C$ are distinct.
\end{assumption}
Assumption~\ref{ass:a4} is a condition on the whole feasible input set, not an additional state constraint in the QP. The nominal-sequence selection rule is an online design choice; the analysis below applies to any feasible $\bar U$ selected before the frozen QP is formed and does not cover iterative re-freezing.

\paragraph{One receding-horizon cycle.} At each real sampling time, update the measured windows, compute $\vz_0=g_\theta(\mX_k)$, select a feasible nominal $\bar U$, roll out the complete model once, freeze $\bar\mA_i,\bar\mB_i$, assemble and solve one convex QP, and apply its first input. At the next sampling time, rebuild the histories and repeat. The fixed-nominal results do not establish convergence or closed-loop guarantees for iterative re-freezing.

\subsection{Prediction matrices and convex quadratic program}

Stack the frozen states and inputs as
\begin{align}
    \mZ&=[(\vz_1^{\mathrm{fr}})^\top,\ldots,(\vz_N^{\mathrm{fr}})^\top]^\top,
    \label{eq:stacked_frozen_states}\\
    \mU&=[\vu_0^\top,\ldots,\vu_{N-1}^\top]^\top.
    \label{eq:stacked_inputs}
\end{align}
The frozen model gives
\begin{equation}
    \mZ=\mF+\mG\mU,
    \label{eq:frozen_prediction_matrices}
\end{equation}
where the block rows of $\mF$ and $\mG$ are denoted by $F_i$ and $G_{i,t}$, respectively:
\begin{align}
    F_i&=\bar\mA_{i-1}\cdots\bar\mA_0\vz_0,
    \label{eq:F_blocks}\\
    G_{i,t}&=\begin{cases}
    \bar\mA_{i-1}\cdots\bar\mA_{t+1}\bar\mB_t,&t<i,\\
    0,&t\ge i.
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:F_G_blocks}
\end{align}
The products are time ordered; no commutativity of the matrices is assumed. Let $\bar C_x=I_N\otimes C_x$, $Y^{\mathrm{ref}}=[(\vx_1^{\mathrm{ref}})^\top,\ldots,(\vx_N^{\mathrm{ref}})^\top]^\top$, and $\bar Q_x=\operatorname{blkdiag}(Q_x,\ldots,Q_x,P_x)$. Define $\bar R$ and $\bar S$ as the corresponding block-diagonal input matrices, and let $\hat D$ satisfy $(\hat D\mU)_0=\vu_0$ and $(\hat D\mU)_i=\vu_i-\vu_{i-1}$ for $i\ge1$. With the input-offset vector $\hat{\mathbf c}=[-\vu_{-1}^\top,0,\ldots,0]^\top$, the frozen cost is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
J_{\mathrm{fr}}(U;\bar U)={}&\norm{\bar C_x\mZ-Y^{\mathrm{ref}}}_{\bar Q_x}^2\\
&+\norm{\mU-\mU^{\mathrm{ref}}}_{\bar R}^2\\
&+\norm{\hat D\mU+\hat{\mathbf c}}_{\bar S}^2.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:frozen_cost}
\end{equation}
Here $\mF\in\R^{Nd}$, $\mG\in\R^{Nd\times Nm}$, $\bar C_x\in\R^{Nn\times Nd}$, and $\hat D\in\R^{Nm\times Nm}$. The first row of $\hat D$ is $[I_m\;0\;\cdots\;0]$ and row $i\ge1$ has $-I_m$ in block column $i-1$ and $I_m$ in block column $i$; thus $\hat{\mathbf c}=[-\vu_{-1}^{\top},0,\ldots,0]^{\top}\in\R^{Nm}$. Empty matrix products in \eqref{eq:F_G_blocks} are identity matrices. For a polyhedral input set $\mathcal U=\{u:A_u u\le b_u\}$, the stacked constraint is $(I_N\otimes A_u)\mU\le\mathbf 1_N\otimes b_u$.

Substitution of \eqref{eq:frozen_prediction_matrices} gives
\begin{equation}
    J_{\mathrm{fr}}(U;\bar U)=\frac12\mU^\top \mathcal H\mU+q^\top\mU+\mathrm{const},
    \label{eq:quadratic_template}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{align}
 \mathcal H&=2\left(\begin{aligned}
 &\mG^\top\bar C_x^\top\bar Q_x\bar C_x\mG\\
 &+\bar R+\hat D^\top\bar S\hat D
\end{aligned}\right),
\label{eq:QP_Hessian}\\
 q&=2f,\qquad f=\begin{aligned}
 &\mG^\top\bar C_x^\top\bar Q_x(\bar C_x\mF-Y^{\mathrm{ref}})\\
 &-\bar R\mU^{\mathrm{ref}}+\hat D^\top\bar S\hat{\mathbf c}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:QP_coefficients}
\end{align}
Every term in $\mathcal H$ is positive semidefinite, so $\mathcal H\succeq0$ and the objective is convex. With a box or other polyhedral input set, the frozen problem is a standard convex QP. Strict convexity and uniqueness require an additional positive-definiteness condition, for example $\bar R\succ0$. When $N=1$, the output term contains only the terminal weight $P_x$ and no intermediate $Q_x$ term.

\subsection{Freezing error and conditional suboptimality}

Fix a candidate $U$ and nominal $\bar U$. Let $\vz_i^{\mathrm{full}}$ be the complete scheduled trajectory, $\vz_i^{\mathrm{fr}}$ the frozen trajectory, and $\bar\vz_i$ the nominal trajectory. For $i=0,\ldots,N-1$, let $U_i^{\mathrm{in}}$ and $\bar U_i^{\mathrm{in}}$ denote the length-$(P-1)$ past-input windows assembled from the common pre-horizon inputs and from $\vu_0,\ldots,\vu_{i-1}$ or $\bar\vu_0,\ldots,\bar\vu_{i-1}$, respectively. Define $\ve_i=\vz_i^{\mathrm{full}}-\vz_i^{\mathrm{fr}}$ and $\vg_i=\vz_i^{\mathrm{fr}}-\bar\vz_i$. Let
\begin{align}
    \delta_u&=\max_{0\le i<N}\norm{\vu_i-\bar\vu_i}_2,
    \label{eq:input_deviation}\\
    \Delta_u&=\max_{0\le i<N}\norm{U_i^{\mathrm{in}}-\bar U_i^{\mathrm{in}}}_2.
\label{eq:input_window_deviation}
\end{align}
For this control comparison only, define the nondecreasing frozen-error envelope
\begin{equation}
 W_i^{\mathrm{fr}}:=\max_{0\le j\le i}\norm{\ve_j}_2.
 \label{eq:frozen_error_maximum}
\end{equation}
Then $\Delta_u\le\sqrt{P-1}\delta_u$ because the past input windows share their initial entries and differ only in shifted future inputs. With $M_B=\norm{\mB_0}_2+g_B$, the nominal drift satisfies
\begin{equation}
    \norm{\vg_i}_2\le G_i:=M_B\delta_u\sum_{t=0}^{i-1}M_A^{i-1-t}.
    \label{eq:nominal_drift}
\end{equation}

The three trajectories have a useful exact first-step identity. Because their initial state and history are common, $\bar\mA_0=\mA(\vc_0^{\mathrm{full}})$ and $\bar\mB_0=\mB(\vchi_0^{\mathrm{full}})$ for every candidate $U$. Consequently,
\begin{equation}
 \vz_1^{\mathrm{full}}=\vz_1^{\mathrm{fr}},\qquad \ve_0=\ve_1=0,
 \label{eq:first_step_freezing_exact}
\end{equation}
even when $U\ne\bar U$. This exactness is not a closed-loop statement.

For a less conservative candidate-specific bound, define $a_i=\norm{\vu_i-\bar\vu_i}_2$, $D_i=\norm{U_i^{\mathrm{in}}-\bar U_i^{\mathrm{in}}}_2$, and
\begin{equation}
 G_0^{\mathrm{st}}=0,\qquad G_{i+1}^{\mathrm{st}}=M_A G_i^{\mathrm{st}}+M_Ba_i,
 \label{eq:stage_nominal_drift}
\end{equation}
so that $\norm{\vg_i}_2\le G_i^{\mathrm{st}}$. Define the history envelope
\begin{equation}
 \widehat G_i:=\max_{\max\{0,i-P+1\}\le j\le i}G_j^{\mathrm{st}},
 \label{eq:stage_history_drift}
\end{equation}
where pre-horizon slots have drift zero. Let $E_0=0$ and recursively set
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 d_i&=L_AL_\phi M_ZD_i
 +(L_AL_\phi\sqrt P M_Z+L_BM_U)\widehat G_i,\\
 E_{i+1}&=\max\{E_i,LE_i+d_i\}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:stage_freezing_bound}
\end{equation}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:a2} and \ref{ass:a4}, $\norm{\ve_i}_2\le E_i$. In particular, $D_0=G_0^{\mathrm{st}}=\widehat G_0=d_0=E_1=0$, which agrees with \eqref{eq:first_step_freezing_exact}. Neither the pointwise input-window deviations nor the stage drifts need be monotone, so no geometric-sum simplification of \eqref{eq:stage_freezing_bound} is made. A proof is given in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.

To make the bound uniform over the complete feasible set, define the fixed-nominal suprema
\begin{align}
\bar\delta_u&:=\sup_{U\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{feas}}}\max_{0\le i<N}\norm{\vu_i-\bar\vu_i}_2,
\label{eq:uniform_pointwise_input_deviation}\\
\bar\Delta_u&:=\sup_{U\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{feas}}}\max_{0\le i<N}\norm{U_i^{\mathrm{in}}-\bar U_i^{\mathrm{in}}}_2.
\label{eq:uniform_input_deviation}
\end{align}
Compactness of $\mathcal U_{\mathrm{feas}}$ makes these quantities finite, and $\bar\Delta_u\le\sqrt{P-1}\bar\delta_u$. Set
\begin{align}
\bar G_N&:=M_B\bar\delta_u\sum_{t=0}^{N-1}M_A^t,
\label{eq:uniform_nominal_drift}\\
\bar\delta_{\mathrm{frz}}&:=\begin{aligned}
 &L_AL_\phi M_Z\bar\Delta_u\\
 &+\left(L_AL_\phi\sqrt{P}M_Z+L_BM_U\right)\bar G_N,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:uniform_freezing_driver}
\end{align}
and, for $0\le i\le N$, with the same empty-sum convention,
\begin{equation}
\bar K_i:=\bar\delta_{\mathrm{frz}}\sum_{t=0}^{i-1}L^{i-1-t}.
\label{eq:uniform_freezing_bound}
\end{equation}
For the fixed candidate under Assumptions~\ref{ass:a2} and \ref{ass:a4}, define
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    \delta_{\mathrm{frz}}={}&L_AL_\phi M_Z\Delta_u\\
    &+\left(L_AL_\phi\sqrt{P}M_Z+L_BM_U\right)G_N.
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:freezing_driver}
\end{equation}
With $L$ from \eqref{eq:prediction_L}, the freezing error obeys
\begin{equation}
    \norm{\ve_i}_2\le K_i:=\delta_{\mathrm{frz}}
    \sum_{t=0}^{i-1}L^{i-1-t},\qquad 0\le i\le N.
    \label{eq:freezing_bound}
\end{equation}
This is a same-input, finite-horizon open-loop comparison. Equivalently, $W_i^{\mathrm{fr}}\le K_i$ because $K_i$ is nondecreasing and bounds every $\norm{\ve_j}_2$ for $j\le i$.

For every $U\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{feas}}$, the candidate-specific quantities satisfy $\delta_u\le\bar\delta_u$, $\Delta_u\le\bar\Delta_u$, and $G_N\le\bar G_N$. Thus $\delta_{\mathrm{frz}}\le\bar\delta_{\mathrm{frz}}$ and the preceding comparison gives $\norm{\ve_i}_2\le K_i\le\bar K_i$.

The stage-dependent bound \eqref{eq:stage_freezing_bound} is the sharper candidate-specific certificate. The simpler $K_i$ bound above is retained as a uniform, conservative certificate because it uses horizon-wide envelopes.

To express the cost difference uniformly, let $M_Y=\norm{C_x}_2M_Z$, $M_{\mathrm{ref}}=\max_{1\le i\le N}\norm{\vx_i^{\mathrm{ref}}}_2$, and $\bar K_i^x=\norm{C_x}_2\bar K_i$. A uniform sufficient bound is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\varepsilon_J={}&2(M_Y+M_{\mathrm{ref}})
 \left(\norm{Q_x}_2\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\bar K_i^x
 +\norm{P_x}_2\bar K_N^x\right)\\
&+\norm{Q_x}_2\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}(\bar K_i^x)^2\\
&+\norm{P_x}_2(\bar K_N^x)^2.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:epsilon_cost}
\end{equation}
It follows from the identity for the difference of two squared weighted norms and from the fact that the input and input-increment terms in \eqref{eq:full_cost} and \eqref{eq:frozen_cost} are identical. More explicitly, let $y_i^{\mathrm{full}}=C_x\vz_i^{\mathrm{full}}$, $y_i^{\mathrm{fr}}=C_x\vz_i^{\mathrm{fr}}$, and $d_i=y_i^{\mathrm{full}}-y_i^{\mathrm{fr}}$. The uniform freezing bound gives $\norm{d_i}_2\le\bar K_i^x$. With $M_Y=\norm{C_x}_2M_Z$ and $M_{\mathrm{ref}}=\max_{1\le i\le N}\norm{\vx_i^{\mathrm{ref}}}_2$, Assumption~\ref{ass:a4} gives $\norm{y_i^{\mathrm{full}}}_2\le M_Y$ and hence $\norm{y_i^{\mathrm{fr}}}_2\le M_Y+\bar K_i^x$. Therefore, for $i<N$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&\left|\norm{y_i^{\mathrm{full}}-\vx_i^{\mathrm{ref}}}_{Q_x}^2-
\norm{y_i^{\mathrm{fr}}-\vx_i^{\mathrm{ref}}}_{Q_x}^2\right|\\
&\quad\le\norm{Q_x}_2\bar K_i^x(2M_Y+\bar K_i^x+2M_{\mathrm{ref}}),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
and the same identity with $P_x$ applies at $i=N$. Summing these bounds and using the common input terms yields \eqref{eq:epsilon_cost}. Because \eqref{eq:uniform_input_deviation} takes the supremum over the entire feasible set and Assumptions~\ref{ass:a2} and \ref{ass:a4} use the same domain constants there, this calculation proves the following proposition.

\begin{proposition}[Uniform objective approximation certificate]
\label{prop:uniform_cost_certificate}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:a2} and \ref{ass:a4},
\begin{equation}
 \sup_{U\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{feas}}}
 \left|J_{\mathrm{full}}(U)-J_{\mathrm{fr}}(U;\bar U)\right|\le\varepsilon_J.
 \label{eq:uniform_cost_certificate}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{theorem}[Conditional cost suboptimality]
\label{thm:suboptimality}
Suppose the complete and frozen problems have the same nonempty feasible input set, and let $U^{\mathrm{fr}}$ and $U^\star$ be global minimizers of the frozen and complete costs, respectively. Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:a2} and \ref{ass:a4},
\begin{equation}
    0\le J_{\mathrm{full}}(U^{\mathrm{fr}})-J_{\mathrm{full}}(U^\star)
    \le2\varepsilon_J.
    \label{eq:suboptimality_bound}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The lower bound follows from global optimality of $U^\star$. For the upper bound,
\begin{align*}
J_{\mathrm{full}}(U^{\mathrm{fr}})
&\le J_{\mathrm{fr}}(U^{\mathrm{fr}};\bar U)+\varepsilon_J\\
&\le J_{\mathrm{fr}}(U^\star;\bar U)+\varepsilon_J\\
&\le J_{\mathrm{full}}(U^\star)+2\varepsilon_J.
\end{align*}
\end{proof}

The comparison is per sampling instant on a common feasible input set with a fixed nominal sequence. If a solver returns $\widetilde U$ satisfying $J_{\mathrm{fr}}(\widetilde U;\bar U)\le J_{\mathrm{fr}}(U^{\mathrm{fr}};\bar U)+\eta$, the same argument gives $J_{\mathrm{full}}(\widetilde U)-J_{\mathrm{full}}(U^\star)\le2\varepsilon_J+\eta$.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
% =========================================================

This paper formulated a history-conditioned scheduled predictor with a temporal lifting, a bounded low-rank history-conditioned transition, and a measurement-dependent input modulation. The scheduled form preserves linear propagation when the scheduling signals are fixed, while its recursive use remains generally nonlinear. The finite-horizon prediction result makes the history-window coupling explicit and gives a conditional open-loop bound under common-history, work-domain, Lipschitz, and residual conditions.

For control, freezing the scheduling matrices along a nominal rollout produces an LTV prediction model and a convex QP with measurement-space tracking costs. The freezing analysis separates nominal drift from the complete-versus-frozen trajectory error. Under a uniform cost approximation and a common feasible set, the frozen global optimizer satisfies a conditional $2\varepsilon_J$ cost bound relative to the global optimizer of the complete scheduled problem.

The results are finite-horizon and open-loop; the uniform work-domain and residual conditions require certification for a concrete system.

\section{Proof Details}
\label{app:proofs}

\subsection*{Scheduling bounds}
For completeness, the softmax Jacobian is $J(\pi)=\operatorname{diag}(\pi)-\pi\pi^\top$. It is positive semidefinite and, by the variance bound, satisfies $\norm{J(\pi)}_2\le1/2$. Integrating the Jacobian along the line segment between two logits gives $\norm{\operatorname{softmax}(a)-\operatorname{softmax}(b)}_2\le\frac12\norm{a-b}_2$. Combining this inequality with the $\tanh$ Lipschitz property, $\norm{\cdot}_1\le\sqrt r\norm{\cdot}_2$, and the rank-one factor normalization gives \eqref{eq:schedule_lipschitz}.

\subsection*{Frozen error bound}
For a fixed candidate $U$ and nominal $\bar U$, the three trajectories are distinct: $\bar\vz_i$ uses $\bar U$ and recomputed schedules, $\vz_i^{\mathrm{fr}}$ uses $U$ and fixed nominal matrices, and $\vz_i^{\mathrm{full}}$ uses $U$ and recomputed schedules. Let $\mX_i^{\mathrm{full}}$ and $\bar\mX_i$ be their recursively shifted measurement windows; let $U_i^{\mathrm{in}}$ and $\bar U_i^{\mathrm{in}}$ be the associated past-input windows; and define $\vc_i^{\mathrm{full}}=C_\phi(\mX_i^{\mathrm{full}},U_i^{\mathrm{in}})$, $\bar\vc_i=C_\phi(\bar\mX_i,\bar U_i^{\mathrm{in}})$, $\vchi_i^{\mathrm{full}}=C_x\vz_i^{\mathrm{full}}$, and $\bar\vchi_i=C_x\bar\vz_i$. Hence $\bar\mA_i=\mA(\bar\vc_i)$ and $\bar\mB_i=\mB(\bar\vchi_i)$. Their differences satisfy
\begin{align}
 \vg_{i+1}&=\bar\mA_i\vg_i+\bar\mB_i(\vu_i-\bar\vu_i),\label{eq:appendix_nominal_drift_recursion}\\
 \ve_{i+1}&=\bar\mA_i\ve_i
 +[\mA(\vc_i^{\mathrm{full}})-\bar\mA_i]\vz_i^{\mathrm{full}}
 +[\mB(\vchi_i^{\mathrm{full}})-\bar\mB_i]\vu_i.
 \label{eq:appendix_three_trajectory_recursions}
\end{align}
Thus $\norm{\vg_i}_2\le G_i$ in \eqref{eq:nominal_drift}. The full and nominal measurement histories differ slotwise by $C_x(\ve_j+\vg_j)$ after the prediction start and are identical before it, so
\begin{equation*}
 \norm{\mX_i^{\mathrm{full}}-\bar\mX_i}_2
 \le\norm{C_x}_2\sqrt P\left(W_i^{\mathrm{fr}}+G_N\right).
\end{equation*}
The input-window difference is at most $\Delta_u$. Assumption~\ref{ass:a2} therefore bounds the context difference by $L_\phi[\sqrt P(W_i^{\mathrm{fr}}+G_N)+\Delta_u]$. The current measured-state difference is at most $\norm{C_x}_2(W_i^{\mathrm{fr}}+G_N)$, so the schedule Lipschitz bound gives
\begin{equation*}
 \norm{\ve_{i+1}}_2\le\delta_{\mathrm{frz}}+LW_i^{\mathrm{fr}},
\end{equation*}
with $\delta_{\mathrm{frz}}$ from \eqref{eq:freezing_driver}; the present manuscript uses $\norm{C_x}_2=1$. The nondecreasing comparison sequence for $W_{i+1}^{\mathrm{fr}}=\max\{W_i^{\mathrm{fr}},\norm{\ve_{i+1}}_2\}$ then proves \eqref{eq:freezing_bound}.

For the stage-dependent refinement, $D_0=G_0^{\mathrm{st}}=\widehat G_0=0$, hence the two full schedules at stage zero equal the nominal schedules and $\ve_1=0$. At stage $i$, the same history argument with $D_i$ and the active-history envelope $\widehat G_i$ gives $\norm{\ve_{i+1}}_2\le LE_i+d_i$. Taking the maximum with $E_i$ proves by induction that $\norm{\ve_i}_2\le E_i$. No monotonicity of $D_i$ or $G_i^{\mathrm{st}}$ is used.

\subsection*{Quadratic program}
Let $\mG_x=\bar C_x\mG$ and $\mW=\bar C_x\mF-Y^{\mathrm{ref}}$. The output term expands as
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    &(\mW+\mG_x\mU)^\top\bar Q_x(\mW+\mG_x\mU)\\
    &\quad=\mU^\top\mG_x^\top\bar Q_x\mG_x\mU\\
    &\qquad+2(\mG_x^\top\bar Q_x\mW)^\top\mU+\mathrm{const}.
    \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The input and input-increment terms expand analogously. Their sum yields \eqref{eq:QP_Hessian} and $q=2f$ in \eqref{eq:QP_coefficients}. Positive semidefiniteness follows because each quadratic coefficient has the form $T^\top MT$ with $M\succeq0$.
