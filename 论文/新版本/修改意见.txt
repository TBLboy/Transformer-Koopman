\section{Real-Time Predictive Control}
\label{sec:control}
% =========================================================

We integrate the rollout-trained scheduled predictor into a receding-horizon controller that runs within the sampling period $T_s=200\,\mathrm{ms}$ of the soft-robot platform.

\subsection{History-Consistent Scheduled Prediction}

At each control instant the controller predicts over a horizon $N$ by propagating the latent and re-scheduling the context from the decoded history,
\begin{equation}
    \vz_{i+1}=\mA(\vc_i)\vz_i+\mB\vu_i,\quad
    \vc_i=C_\phi(\hat{\mX}_i,\mU^{\mathrm{in}}_i),\quad
    \hat{\vx}_i=\mC\vz_i ,
    \label{eq:scheduled_predictor}
\end{equation}
where $\hat{\mX}_i$ is the rolled-out history. This \emph{context-re-scheduled, latent-propagated} structure (RTI-SP) never re-runs the full Transformer lifting encoder inside the horizon. The alternative---re-encoding the history at every prediction stage (RTI-RE)---is more accurate in principle but costs one encoder evaluation per stage.

\subsection{One-Iteration RTI}

We solve the receding-horizon problem
\begin{equation}
    \min_{\vu_{0:N-1}}\ \sum_{i=1}^{N}\|\hat{\vx}_i-\vx_i^{\mathrm{ref}}\|_{\mQ}^2+\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\big(\|\vu_i\|_{\mR}^2+\|\vu_i-\vu_{i-1}\|_{\mS}^2\big)
    \label{eq:mpc_cost}
\end{equation}
subject to $\vu_i\in[\vu_{\min},\vu_{\max}]$, using a single real-time-iteration (RTI) SQP step per sampling instant \cite{diehl2005realtime, gros2020rti}: (i) warm-start from the shifted previous solution; (ii) roll out the nominal trajectory \eqref{eq:scheduled_predictor}; (iii) linearize to obtain $\delta\vxi_{i+1}=\mF_i\delta\vxi_i+\mG_i\delta\vu_i$; (iv) solve one quadratic program over $\delta\vu_{0:N-1}$; and (v) apply only the first input. Proposition~\ref{prop:rti} bounds the second-order linearization remainder of the model used in the RTI step; the adequacy of a single iteration is assessed empirically against the matched exact scheduled NMPC oracle, and we make no global SQP convergence claim.

Three points follow. First, one-iteration RTI-SP tracks the \emph{matched} exact scheduled oracle to within about $1\%$ at $N=5$, i.e. the RTI approximation introduces no meaningful tracking degradation. Second, RTI-SP is roughly $2.3\times$ faster than RTI-RE at the median and $2.1\times$ at $p95$, because it avoids a full Transformer lifting-encoder evaluation per stage, and its $p95$ latency ($120$~ms) is well below the $\sim200$~ms soft-robot sampling period. Third, the small negative $\Delta$ should not be read as RTI outperforming exact NMPC; it is attributable to numerical optimization, warm-starting, finite simulation length, solver tolerance, and receding-horizon trajectory differences. Accordingly, the exact oracle is not a strict RMSE upper bound, which is why Proposition~\ref{prop:rti} is assessed by its own remainder diagnostic (Section~\ref{subsec:diagnostics}) rather than by this comparison. We note that a longer horizon ($N=8$) tracks slightly better but exceeds the sampling period ($p95=215$~ms) and is therefore not used.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
% =========================================================

This paper presented Transformer-Koopman, a framework for learning history-conditioned Koopman models of controlled nonlinear systems from finite history windows. Because the measured state need not reveal the system's internal dynamics, a patch-based Transformer encoder first maps the history to a structured, state-preserving lifted state. Because a single global lifted transition would then still require all history-dependent regimes to share one evolution law, the lifted transition is additionally modulated by the history through a rank-$r$ term $\mA(\vc)=\mA_0+\mU\,\mathrm{diag}(\gamma_\psi(\vc))\mV^\top$ ($r=2$), which confines history dependence to a structured correction of a shared nominal operator while keeping the propagation conditional-linear. We identified a training--deployment inconsistency between one-step-trained history-conditioned dynamics and the recursively generated latent states seen at deployment, showed that it is not repaired by spectral stabilization, and resolved it with deployment-consistent scheduled training. The rollout-trained scheduled predictor was integrated into a one-iteration RTI-MPC that runs in real time and matches a matched exact scheduled NMPC oracle to within about $1\%$. A finite-horizon theory---finite-history Markovization, latent rollout error propagation, a bounded prediction-error theorem, a physical-space corollary, and a local RTI approximation result---was established and supported by empirical diagnostics. Hardware validation on the soft-robot platform across multi-setpoint, circular, figure-eight, unseen-trajectory, and speed-shift regimes, together with closed-loop baselines against a fixed (time-invariant) Transformer-Koopman controller, remains the immediate next step.
