\title{Transformer--Koopman: History-Conditioned Scheduled Prediction and Frozen-LTV Model Predictive Control}
\author{Bolin Tao}
\markboth{IEEE Journal Draft}{Tao: Transformer--Koopman}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Finite measurement histories can distinguish dynamical conditions that a single observation cannot, but history-dependent prediction generally couples future dynamics to the candidate control inputs. This paper combines a measurement-preserving temporal Transformer lift with low-rank transition scheduling driven by recursively updated history. A nonredundant augmented state carries the lifted coordinates and the measurement and input caches. One nonlinear nominal rollout then supplies a frozen linear time-varying model for a single convex quadratic program at each sample. A reference-relative objective and a shift of the returned sequence with a reference tail connect successive frozen problems. We give explicit common-contraction conditions for the scheduled dynamics using a Schur skeleton and modulation-derivative budgets; the measurement re-encoding residual remains an independent condition on the chosen lift and system. Under these contraction, residual, and reference conditions, a direct analysis of the implemented frozen optimal values yields practical exponential tracking. The bound separates freezing, re-encoding, reference defect, and certified feasible QP suboptimality, without requiring a second online nonlinear rollout or terminal optimization.
\end{abstract}
\begin{IEEEkeywords}
Koopman modeling, temporal Transformer, history-conditioned prediction, low-rank scheduling, convex model predictive control, practical exponential tracking.
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
Model predictive control (MPC) selects control inputs by comparing their predicted effects over a finite horizon. Its prediction model must therefore capture the response of a nonlinear system to candidate inputs while remaining tractable for repeated online optimization. When the model is identified from input--output data, these requirements concern both the information used to initialize predictions and the structure used to propagate them \cite{ljung1999system,nelles2020nonlinear,forgione2021dynonet}. An informative representation is needed to distinguish different dynamical conditions, whereas a control-oriented evolution model is needed to evaluate their response to future inputs.

Koopman-based prediction offers a way to impose such an evolution structure: nonlinear observables provide lifted coordinates in which a matrix model approximates the dynamics. The underlying Koopman operator is linear but generally infinite-dimensional \cite{koopman1931hamiltonian,mezic2005spectral,brunton2022modern}; a computational predictor must instead use a finite set of observables. Extended dynamic mode decomposition (EDMD) estimates the corresponding finite-dimensional operator approximation from data \cite{williams2015data}. For control, the additional task is to represent the effect of externally chosen inputs. Dynamic mode decomposition with control separates state and input contributions, while controlled EDMD constructions yield lifted predictors with an explicit input channel \cite{proctor2016dmdc,korda2018linear}. With a linear input-affine predictor, quadratic costs and linear constraints can then lead to a convex MPC problem. This control advantage motivates the structure, but its usefulness still depends on finding suitable observables. Neural lifting methods address that choice by learning the coordinates together with their evolution rather than fixing the entire dictionary beforehand \cite{takeishi2017koopman,lusch2018deep,otto2019linearly,han2020deep}.

Learning the observables, however, cannot recover information that is absent from their inputs. If different physical states produce the same current measurement but different subsequent responses, a lift of that measurement alone cannot distinguish them. Past measurements and applied inputs can supply additional information about the preceding evolution. This motivates history-based representations: delay-coordinate approaches augment observables with past measurements \cite{kamb2020timedelay,das2019delay}, and recurrent or temporal autoencoders learn features from sequences for prediction and control \cite{han2024robust,shenoy2025temporal}. Transformer-based lifting extends sequence feature extraction to Koopman-inspired forecasting, as in DeepKoopFormer \cite{forootani2026deepkoopformer}. These approaches motivate using history to initialize the predictor; they do not require the physical state to be reconstructed explicitly.

History can also condition the evolution model itself. While a fixed transition matrix offers a simple prediction structure, allowing its coefficients to depend on recent data provides additional modeling flexibility. Koopa employs context-dependent operators for nonstationary forecasting \cite{liu2023koopa}, while MamKO generates matrices from historical data for modeling and predictive control \cite{li2025mamko}. These studies motivate distinguishing history-dependent initialization from history-conditioned state propagation. In receding-horizon prediction, however, the scheduling history must evolve with the predicted trajectory. The resulting coefficients depend on predicted measurements and candidate inputs, so conditional linearity at a fixed context does not preserve convexity of the full MPC problem.

To retain a quadratic program (QP), the coefficient sequence can be computed along a nominal rollout and held fixed while optimizing the candidate inputs. Quasi-linear parameter-varying MPC similarly exploits fixed scheduling sequences to form tractable subproblems, including iterative QP schemes \cite{cisneros2016qlmpc,cisneros2020qlpv}. Here we consider the specific policy of one nominal rollout and one frozen QP per sample, rather than iterating to update the schedules. The resulting approximation must be analyzed as part of the control law: at the next sample, new measurements change the lifted initialization and a new nominal rollout changes the prediction matrices. A decrease derived for one unchanged frozen model therefore does not establish a decrease across the problems actually solved. Likewise, results for real-time iteration concern a particular optimization update and cannot be transferred solely because both methods use a quadratic subproblem \cite{diehl2005realtime,gros2020rti}. The question is how the history-dependent model, the returned input sequence, and the next nominal rollout can be coupled to support a cross-sample tracking argument.

This paper develops a history-conditioned Transformer--Koopman predictor and a single-freeze MPC scheme. A temporal Transformer initializes the lifted state from measured history, while recursively updated measurement and input histories schedule low-rank transition corrections and measurement-dependent input modulation. At each sample, one nominal rollout determines the frozen linear time-varying prediction model used in a convex QP. The controller combines augmented-state tracking, reference-relative input increments, and a shifted input sequence with a reference tail. Under explicit common-contraction, re-encoding residual, and reference conditions, we establish practical exponential tracking by directly comparing the optimal values of consecutive frozen problems.

The main contributions are as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{A recursive history-conditioned predictor:} we combine a measurement-preserving Transformer lift with low-rank scheduling in a nonredundant augmented state, specifying how history initializes the features and conditions their subsequent evolution.
\item \emph{A single-freeze control law:} we couple one nominal rollout and one convex QP through a complete augmented tracking cost, reference-relative input increments, and a shift of the returned sequence with a reference tail.
\item \emph{Conditional closed-loop tracking analysis:} we construct sufficient common-contraction budgets and establish practical exponential tracking for the specified controller by comparing its successive frozen optimal values. The bound accounts separately for freezing, re-encoding, reference defects, and certified feasible QP suboptimality.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Preliminaries and Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:prelim}
\subsection{Koopman-Based Prediction}
\label{subsec:koopman_prelim}
Consider the discrete-time nonlinear system
\begin{equation}
\vs_{k+1}=f(\vs_k,\vu_k),\qquad \vx_k=h(\vs_k),
\label{eq:nonlinear_system}
\end{equation}
where $\vs_k\in\mathcal S\subseteq\R^{n_s}$ is the physical state and $\vu_k\in\R^m$ is the control input. The available measurement $\vx_k\in\R^n$ is obtained through the measurement map $h:\mathcal S\to\R^n$. The state transition map $f:\mathcal S\times\R^m\to\mathcal S$ governs the evolution of $\vs_k$ under $\vu_k$. Only $\vx_k$ is available to the predictor and the controller; we therefore learn the predictor from measured input--output trajectories and use finite observation histories to capture temporal information not resolved by individual measurements.

For a fixed input $\vu$, the controlled Koopman operator acts on an observable $\varphi:\mathcal S\to\R$ as \cite{koopman1931hamiltonian,mezic2005spectral,brunton2022modern}
\begin{equation}
(\mathcal K_{\vu}\varphi)(\vs)=\varphi(f(\vs,\vu)).
\label{eq:koopman_operator}
\end{equation}
The operator is linear with respect to observables:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal K_{\vu}(\alpha\varphi+\beta\psi)=\alpha\mathcal K_{\vu}\varphi+\beta\mathcal K_{\vu}\psi.
\label{eq:koopman_linearity}
\end{equation}

Finite-dimensional Koopman models approximate this evolution using a selected set of observables. Since only $\vx=h(\vs)$ is measured, we construct the observables from $\vx$; each component $g_j(\vx)$ corresponds to the physical-state observable $g_j\circ h$. An instantaneous lifting map takes the form
\begin{equation}
\vz_k=g(\vx_k)=[g_1(\vx_k),\ldots,g_d(\vx_k)]^\top\in\R^d,
\label{eq:instantaneous_lifting}
\end{equation}
with the input-affine lifted approximation
\begin{equation}
\vz_{k+1}\approx\mA\vz_k+\mB\vu_k.
\label{eq:linear_lifted}
\end{equation}
Here $\mA\in\R^{d\times d}$ approximates the evolution of the selected observables, and $\mB\in\R^{d\times m}$ represents the control-input contribution. Together they define a finite-dimensional approximation of the controlled Koopman evolution in the chosen lifted coordinates.

To incorporate temporal information, we collect the $P_h$ most recent measurements into the measurement-history window
\begin{equation}
\mX_k=\col(\vx_{k-P_h+1},\ldots,\vx_k)\in\R^{P_h n},
\label{eq:measurement_window}
\end{equation}
where $P_h\ge2$ is the window length and $\col(\cdot)$ denotes vertical stacking. We extend the lifting map to
\begin{equation}
\vz_k=G_\theta(\mX_k)=\col(\vx_k,\psi_\theta(\mX_k))\in\R^d,
\label{eq:state_preserving_lifting}
\end{equation}
where $d=n+n_\psi$. The lift retains the current measurement $\vx_k$ as its leading block, so the readout is fixed:
\begin{equation}
\vx_k=C_x\vz_k,\qquad C_x=[I_n\ 0].
\label{eq:state_readout}
\end{equation}
This representation incorporates recent measurements into the lifted state. The transition model may additionally depend on the recent trajectory context, as developed in Section~\ref{sec:model}.

\subsection{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:problem}
The available training data comprise recorded measurement--input trajectories
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal D &= \{\tau^{(j)}\}_{j=1}^{N_{\rm tr}},\\
\tau^{(j)} &= \{(\vx_t^{(j)},\vu_t^{(j)})\}_{t=0}^{T_j},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:training_data}
\end{equation}
where $N_{\rm tr}$ is the number of recorded trajectories and $T_j$ is the last time index of the $j$th trajectory.
For each admissible index $k$, the measured window $\mX_k$ is defined in~\eqref{eq:measurement_window}, and the past-input history is
\begin{equation}
v_k = \col(\vu_{k-P_h+1},\ldots,\vu_{k-1}) \in \R^{(P_h-1)m}.
\label{eq:past_input_history}
\end{equation}
The current input $\vu_k$ is applied after the measured window and is therefore not part of $v_k$.

Given the measured window $\mX_k$, the past-input history $v_k$, and a candidate input sequence $\vu_{k:k+N-1}$, the prediction task is to estimate $\vx_{k+1},\ldots,\vx_{k+N}$, where $N\ge1$ is the prediction horizon, which is also the horizon of the receding-horizon controller in Section~\ref{sec:control}. The predictor should exploit the measured window to initialize the lifted representation and support recursive multi-step prediction. The learned predictor is then used for reference tracking subject to input constraints $\vu_k\in\mathcal U$, where $\mathcal U=\{u:H_uu\leq h_u\}$ is a nonempty compact convex polytope.

The objective of this work is to develop a history-conditioned lifted predictor and a computationally tractable receding-horizon controller, and to establish a practical exponential tracking guarantee for the implemented closed loop under suitable conditions.

\section{History-Conditioned Scheduled Koopman Modeling}
\label{sec:model}
\subsection{History-Conditioned Koopman Predictor}
\label{sec:temporal_lifting}
To construct the history-conditioned predictor formulated in Section~\ref{sec:prelim}, we first specify the temporal encoder used in the lifting map $G_\theta$ in~\eqref{eq:state_preserving_lifting}. Given the measured window $\mX_k$ in~\eqref{eq:measurement_window}, arrange its successive measurement blocks into a $P_h\times n$ array and partition it into $N_p=P_h/p$ nonoverlapping temporal patches of length $p$, where $p$ divides $P_h$. Let $\mX_k^{(j)}\in\R^{p\times n}$ denote the $j$th patch. Each patch is mapped to a token through a shared affine embedding,
\begin{equation}
\vphi_k^{(j)}=\mW_p\operatorname{vec}(\mX_k^{(j)})+\vb_p\in\R^{d_{\rm model}},
\label{eq:patch_embedding}
\end{equation}
where $j=1,\ldots,N_p$, and $\operatorname{vec}(\mX_k^{(j)})\in\R^{pn}$ stacks the $p$ measurement vectors in the $j$th patch into a single column. The embedding parameters $\mW_p\in\R^{d_{\rm model}\times pn}$ and $\vb_p\in\R^{d_{\rm model}}$ are shared across patches. Sinusoidal positional encodings $\vp^{(j)}\in\R^{d_{\rm model}}$ \cite{vaswani2017attention} are added to retain temporal order. An $L_T$-layer Transformer encoder then produces
\begin{equation}
\mH_k=\operatorname{TransformerEncoder}\left(\{\vphi_k^{(j)}+\vp^{(j)}\}_{j=1}^{N_p}\right).
\label{eq:transformer_encoder}
\end{equation}
Here $\mH_k\in\R^{N_p\times d_{\rm model}}$ contains the contextualized patch representations, with $\mH_k^{(j)}\in\R^{d_{\rm model}}$ denoting the representation of the $j$th patch. Mean pooling followed by a linear projection gives
\begin{equation}
\psi_\theta(\mX_k)=\mW_h\left(\frac1{N_p}\sum_{j=1}^{N_p}\mH_k^{(j)}\right)+\vb_h,
\label{eq:history_features}
\end{equation}
where $\psi_\theta(\mX_k)\in\R^{d-n}$, $\mW_h\in\R^{(d-n)\times d_{\rm model}}$, and $\vb_h\in\R^{d-n}$. Together with~\eqref{eq:state_preserving_lifting}, this specifies the history-dependent lifting map $G_\theta$, which initializes the lifted state from the measured window. Recursive multi-step prediction, however, requires two further components: a scheduling history that is carried along the predicted trajectory, so that the transition remains conditioned on history beyond the initial sample, and a transition model whose coefficients depend on this history. We carry the measurement and input histories in an augmented state and let them enter the transition through low-rank corrections to a shared nominal model. The following paragraphs specify these components, and Definition~\ref{def:history_scheduled_predictor} collects them into the recursive predictor.

To carry the scheduling history along the predicted trajectory, define the measurement cache and the augmented state
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
a_k&=\col(\vx_{k-P_h+1},\ldots,\vx_{k-1}),\\
q_k&=\col(\vz_k,a_k,v_k)\in\R^{s_q},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:augmented_state}
\end{equation}
where the measurement cache $a_k$ collects the $P_h-1$ past measurements of the measured window $\mX_k$ in~\eqref{eq:measurement_window}, omitting the current $\vx_k$ already carried in $\vz_k$, and $v_k$ is the past-input history~\eqref{eq:past_input_history}. The augmented state $q_k$ stacks the lifted state with both caches and has dimension $s_q=d+(P_h-1)(n+m)$. For $q=\col(\vz,a,v)$, the reconstructed window is $\mX(q)=\col(a,C_x\vz)\in\R^{P_hn}$, where $C_x\vz$ recovers the current measurement from the lifted state through~\eqref{eq:state_readout}, and the scheduling context is
\begin{equation}
\vc(q)=C_\phi\bigl(\mX(q),v\bigr)\in\R^{d_c},
\label{eq:recursive_context}
\end{equation}
where $C_\phi:\R^{P_hn+(P_h-1)m}\to\R^{d_c}$ is the context encoder. At a measured sample, where $\vz_k=G_\theta(\mX_k)$ and the caches are taken from recorded data, $\mX(q_k)=\mX_k$ by~\eqref{eq:state_readout}; along a predicted trajectory, the reconstructed window is instead formed from the propagated state, so that $\vc$ remains a function of the state that $F$ propagates rather than an external signal. Thus, the scheduling context depends on the reconstructed window and past inputs, but not on the current decision input.

The scheduled transition and input matrices are
\begin{align}
\mA(\vc)&=\mA_0+\mU_A\operatorname{diag}(\gamma_A(\vc))\mV_A^\top,
\label{eq:scheduled_A}\\
\mB(\vx)&=\mB_0+\mU_B\operatorname{diag}(\gamma_B(\vx))\mV_B^\top,
\label{eq:scheduled_B}
\end{align}
where $\mA_0\in\R^{d\times d}$ and $\mB_0\in\R^{d\times m}$ are the nominal lifted-state transition and input matrices, respectively. The operator $\operatorname{diag}(\cdot)$ forms a diagonal matrix from a vector. The matrices $\mU_A,\mV_A\in\R^{d\times r_A}$ and $\mU_B\in\R^{d\times r_B}$, $\mV_B\in\R^{m\times r_B}$ are learnable low-rank factors that specify the correction directions for $\mA_0$ and $\mB_0$, with $r_A$ and $r_B$ denoting the respective rank bounds of the corrections. The scheduling vectors $\gamma_A(\vc)\in\R^{r_A}$ and $\gamma_B(\vx)\in\R^{r_B}$, evaluated at the context $\vc$ and the current measurement $\vx=C_x\vz$, determine the signed magnitudes of the corresponding rank-one correction components. The scheduling functions are parameterized by
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\gamma_{\chi,j}(y)={}&g_\chi\left[\operatorname{softmax}(W_\chi^\pi y+b_\chi^\pi)\right]_j\\
&{}\cdot\tanh\left([W_\chi y+b_\chi]_j\right),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:scheduling_function}
\end{equation}
where $\chi\in\{A,B\}$ indexes the scheduling branch, $j=1,\ldots,r_\chi$, and $y=\vc$ for $\chi=A$ or $y=\vx$ for $\chi=B$. The matrices $W_\chi^\pi,W_\chi\in\R^{r_\chi\times d_y}$ and vectors $b_\chi^\pi,b_\chi\in\R^{r_\chi}$ are learnable parameters, with $d_y=d_c$ for $\chi=A$ and $d_y=n$ for $\chi=B$. The softmax term distributes the scheduling weights across the low-rank components, while the hyperbolic tangent determines their signed modulation. The nonnegative gain $g_\chi$ bounds the magnitude of each scheduling coefficient. If each factor column has Euclidean norm at most one, it follows that $\norm{\mA(\vc)-\mA_0}_2\leq g_A$ and $\norm{\mB(\vx)-\mB_0}_2\leq g_B$. Here $\norm{\cdot}_2$ denotes the spectral norm for matrices and the Euclidean norm for vectors. The gains $g_A$ and $g_B$ therefore serve as explicit correction budgets on the scheduled model.

\begin{definition}[History-Conditioned Scheduled Koopman Predictor]
\label{def:history_scheduled_predictor}
Let $G_\theta$ be the lifting map~\eqref{eq:state_preserving_lifting}, let $\vc(\cdot)$ be the recursive context~\eqref{eq:recursive_context}, and let $\mA(\cdot)$ and $\mB(\cdot)$ be the scheduled matrices~\eqref{eq:scheduled_A}--\eqref{eq:scheduling_function}. The history-conditioned scheduled Koopman predictor initializes the augmented state from the observed histories as
\begin{equation}
q_k^E=\col(G_\theta(\mX_k),a_k,v_k),
\label{eq:encoded_initialization}
\end{equation}
where $a_k$ is the measurement cache defined in~\eqref{eq:augmented_state} and $v_k$ is the past-input history defined in~\eqref{eq:past_input_history}. The superscript $E$ denotes an encoder-initialized state, and we will use $P$ to denote model-propagated states in what follows. Starting from $q_k^E$, the augmented state is propagated according to $q^+=F(q,\vu)$, where $F:\R^{s_q}\times\R^m\to\R^{s_q}$ is defined by
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
F(q,\vu)=\col\bigl(&\mA(\vc(q))\vz+\mB(C_x\vz)\vu,\\
&S_xa+E_xC_x\vz,\ S_uv+E_u\vu\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:recursive_predictor}
\end{equation}
The first block propagates the lifted state, while the remaining blocks update the histories used for subsequent scheduling: $S_x$ and $S_u$ discard the oldest entry of the respective cache, and $E_x$ and $E_u$ append the current measurement $C_x\vz$ and the applied input $\vu$, so that the next context $\vc(q^+)$ is evaluated on the predicted window.\envend
\end{definition}

The encoder is evaluated once per control sample to form $q_k^E$. Predictions over the horizon are then obtained by iterating $F$ on this initial state, without re-encoding the predicted windows. Consequently, the model-propagated state need not coincide with the state encoded from the next measurement. This discrepancy is written as
\begin{equation}
q_{k+1}^E=F(q_k^E,\vu_k)+w_k.
\label{eq:reencoding_residual}
\end{equation}
The cache blocks of $F$ reproduce exact bookkeeping of applied inputs and measurements, so the residual satisfies $w_k=\col(r_k,0,0)$ and lies entirely in the lifted block. It captures two sources of discrepancy: the one-step measurement prediction error and the mismatch between the propagated features and the encoder output on the realized window. The explicit decomposition is provided in Appendix~\ref{app:construction}.

\subsection{Model Learning and Contractive Design}
\label{sec:training}
We train the predictor of Section~\ref{sec:temporal_lifting} in two stages. One-step prediction first fits the lifting map and nominal dynamics. Recursive rollouts then train the scheduled model over the prediction horizon used by the controller.

In the first stage, both scheduling corrections are disabled. We jointly optimize $G_\theta$, $\mA_0$, and $\mB_0$, with $\mA_0$ constrained to be Schur stable. The nominal prediction $\widetilde\vz_{k+1}^0=\mA_0\vz_k^E+\mB_0\vu_k$ is fitted by minimizing
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\mathcal L_{\rm init}=\sum_{k\in\mathcal I_1}\bigl[&
\norm{C_x\widetilde\vz_{k+1}^0-\vx_{k+1}}_2^2\\
&+\alpha_0\norm{\widetilde\vz_{k+1}^0-\vz_{k+1}^E}_2^2\bigr],
\end{split}
\label{eq:training_one_step_objects}
\end{equation}
where $\alpha_0>0$ is a weighting coefficient and $\mathcal I_1$ indexes one-step samples $(\mX_k,\vu_k,\mX_{k+1})$ drawn from individual recorded trajectories.

In the second stage, $\mA_0$ and $\mB_0$ are fixed, and both schedules are activated. We jointly train the lifting map, context encoder, low-rank factors, and scheduling heads under the constraints specified below. Initialize $q_{k|k}^P=q_k^E$ from recorded histories and propagate $q_{k+i+1|k}^P=F(q_{k+i|k}^P,\vu_{k+i})$ for $i=0,\ldots,H_R-1$. We set $H_R=N$ so that training and control use the same prediction horizon. With $\vz_{k+i|k}^P$ denoting the lifted-state block of $q_{k+i|k}^P$, minimize
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\mathcal L_R=\sum_{k\in\mathcal I_R}\sum_{i=1}^{H_R}\omega_i\bigl[&
\norm{C_x\vz_{k+i|k}^P-\vx_{k+i}}_2^2\\
&+\alpha_R\norm{\vz_{k+i|k}^P-\vz_{k+i}^E}_2^2\bigr],
\end{split}
\label{eq:joint_training_loss}
\end{equation}
where $\omega_i=1/H_R$ and $\alpha_R>0$. The set $\mathcal I_R$ indexes multi-step samples comprising the initial histories $(\mX_k,v_k)$, recorded inputs $\vu_{k:k+H_R-1}$, and subsequent measurements $\vx_{k+1},\ldots,\vx_{k+H_R}$ from a single trajectory. The prediction branch uses $G_\theta$ only at initialization; subsequent contexts use reconstructed windows and recorded inputs. The target states are encoded from measured windows formed by successively shifting $\mX_k$ and appending these measurements. In both stages, gradients pass through every encoder evaluation in the losses, and $C_x$ remains fixed. The deployed predictor is deterministic, with dropout disabled and normalization fixed.

In the second stage, we constrain the scheduled corrections using a fixed contraction metric. With the corrections disabled, the augmented predictor has the state matrix
\begin{equation}
M_0=\begin{bmatrix}\mA_0&0&0\\E_xC_x&S_x&0\\0&0&S_u\end{bmatrix}.
\label{eq:lyapunov_skeleton}
\end{equation}
Since $\mA_0$ is Schur stable and the cache shifts $S_x,S_u$ are nilpotent, $M_0$ is Schur stable. We therefore choose $\Pi\succ0$ as the unique solution of the discrete Lyapunov equation
\begin{equation*}
M_0^T\Pi M_0-\Pi=-I.
\end{equation*}
Both $M_0$ and $\Pi$ remain fixed throughout the second stage.

The fixed metric $\Pi$ defines the weighted squared norm $\norm{e}_\Pi^2=e^\top\Pi e$ and the analysis domain $\mathcal Q=\{q:\norm{q}_\Pi\le M\}$, where $M>0$. The induced matrix norm $\norm{L}_\Pi=\norm{\Pi^{1/2}L\Pi^{-1/2}}_2$ gives the nominal contraction rate $\lambda_0=\norm{M_0}_\Pi<1$. On $\mathcal Q$, let $L_c$ bound the Euclidean derivative of the context map $q\mapsto\vc(q)$. For each scheduling branch $\chi\in\{A,B\}$, define
\begin{equation}
\kappa_\chi=\norm{W_\chi}_2+\tfrac12\sqrt{r_\chi}\norm{W_\chi^\pi}_2,\qquad L_\chi=g_\chi\kappa_\chi.
\label{eq:Lipschitz_constants}
\end{equation}
During the second stage, we constrain each factor column to have Euclidean norm at most one and enforce $\kappa_\chi\le\bar\kappa_\chi$ and $L_c\le\bar L_c$ uniformly over the allowed parameters on $\mathcal Q$. Using these fixed finite bounds, we select $g_A,g_B$ by~\eqref{eq:gain_choice} and keep them fixed. Appendix~\ref{app:construction} provides sufficient head-norm, MLP, and attention bounds for enforcing these constraints.

\begin{proposition}[Constructible contractive model class]
\label{prop:contractive_class}
Fix a Schur skeleton~\eqref{eq:lyapunov_skeleton}, $\mB_0$, a compact input polytope $\mathcal U$, and a trial ball $\mathcal Q$. Assume factor-column norms at most one and uniform bounds $\bar\kappa_\chi,\bar L_c$ over the allowed scheduling parameters. For a chosen lift and measured system, assume $\norm{w_t}_\Pi\le\bar w$ for every measured transition with $q_t^E\in\mathcal Q$ and $\vu_t\in\mathcal U$. Let $b_0^{\rm drv}$ bound the nominal input drive. If the ball covers the initial encoded-state set and
\begin{equation}
\Delta=(1-\lambda_0)M-b_0^{\rm drv}-\bar w>0,
\label{eq:radius_margin}
\end{equation}
then the explicit positive-gain choices in \eqref{eq:gain_choice} yield uniform same-input contraction of the complete model and every frozen model generated by a nominal point in $\mathcal Q$, with common bounds over the allowed scheduling parameters. Their rates $\lambda,\lambda_{\rm fr}<1$ and drive bound $b_u$ satisfy
\begin{equation}
\lambda M+b_u+\bar w<M,\qquad \lambda_{\rm fr}M+b_u<M.
\label{eq:invariant_radius}
\end{equation}
Both modulation gains can be nonzero. Nonconstant heads and effective factor directions give genuinely history-dependent members of the class.

For $q_{k|k}^P=q_k^E\in\mathcal Q$ and identical prescribed inputs in $\mathcal U$, the prediction error satisfies
\begin{equation*}
\norm{q_{k+i}^E-q_{k+i|k}^P}_\Pi\le\bar w\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}\lambda^j,\qquad i\ge1.
\end{equation*}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Schur stability gives the common Lyapunov norm. The full derivative perturbation includes the direct correction, the derivative of the history schedule acting on $\vz$, and the derivative of $\mB(C_x\vz)\vu$. Their bounds give $\norm{D_qF-M_0}_\Pi\le\nu_{\rm bd}$; the frozen bound omits the schedule derivatives. Using $\kappa_\chi\le\bar\kappa_\chi$ and $L_c\le\bar L_c$ makes the bounds uniform over the allowed parameters. With the ball and these upper bounds fixed, the perturbation budgets are linear in the common gain scale. The strict choice \eqref{eq:gain_choice} leaves positive contraction and radius margins. The radius inequalities first keep the complete and frozen predictions in $\mathcal Q$. The residual bound then keeps the next encoded state in $\mathcal Q$, so domain invariance follows by induction. The detailed bounds and a nonconstant, encoding-consistent periodic example are given in Appendix~\ref{app:construction}.

For identical prescribed inputs, combining~\eqref{eq:reencoding_residual} with same-input contraction bounds each next prediction error by $\lambda$ times the current error plus $\bar w$. Iterating from zero initial prediction error yields the stated bound.
\end{proof}

\section{Frozen-LTV Model Predictive Control}
\label{sec:control}
\subsection{Frozen-LTV MPC Formulation}
\label{subsec:frozen_mpc}
{\color{blue}The history-conditioned predictor developed in Section~\ref{sec:model} supplies the state-transition model for receding-horizon control. Since its scheduling matrices depend on the evolving predicted state, optimizing the complete recursion would lead to a nonlinear finite-horizon problem. We instead freeze the scheduling coefficients along one nominal rollout at each sampling instant, obtaining a quadratic program with fixed prediction matrices.}

{\color{blue}At sample $k$, construct $q_k^E$ by~\eqref{eq:encoded_initialization} and use a feasible nominal input sequence $\bar U_k=\col(\bar\vu_{0|k},\ldots,\bar\vu_{N-1|k})\in\mathcal U^N$. This sequence determines the frozen coefficients but is not a decision variable of the current QP. The initial sequence $\bar U_0$ is chosen from $\mathcal U^N$. At each subsequent sampling instant, the nominal sequence is formed by shifting the input sequence returned by the previous MPC solve and appending the reference input. The complete-model rollout is}
\begin{equation}
\bar q_{0|k}=q_k^E,\qquad
\bar q_{i+1|k}=F(\bar q_{i|k},\bar\vu_{i|k}),\quad 0\le i<N,
\label{eq:nominal_rollout}
\end{equation}
{\color{blue}Let $E_z=\col(I_d,0,0)\in\R^{s_q\times d}$, so that $E_z^Tq=\vz$. Along the nominal trajectory, evaluate $\bar\mA_{i|k}=\mA(\vc(\bar q_{i|k}))$ and $\bar\mB_{i|k}=\mB(C_xE_z^T\bar q_{i|k})$. Holding these matrices fixed for all candidate inputs gives the linear time-varying prediction model}
\begin{align}
p_{0|k}&=q_k^E,\qquad
p_{i+1|k}=M_{i|k}p_{i|k}+D_{i|k}\vu_{i|k},
\label{eq:frozen_model}\\
\intertext{where}
M_{i|k}&=
\begin{bmatrix}
\bar\mA_{i|k} & 0 & 0\\
E_xC_x & S_x & 0\\
0 & 0 & S_u
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
D_{i|k}=\col(\bar\mB_{i|k},0,E_u).\nonumber
\end{align}

{\color{blue}For fixed coefficients, the predicted states depend affinely on $U=\col(\vu_{0|k},\ldots,\vu_{N-1|k})\in\R^{Nm}$. In particular, $p_{i|k}(\bar U_k)=\bar q_{i|k}$ for $i=0,\ldots,N$: the frozen model exactly reproduces its nominal trajectory. This is coefficient freezing, not a Jacobian linearization of $F$; predictions for other input sequences need not coincide with the complete recursion.}

{\color{blue}Let $\{(q_t^r,\vu_t^r)\}_{t\ge0}$ be a prescribed absolute-time reference in augmented coordinates, with $\vu_{-1}^r$ given. The matrix $E_{\rm prev}\in\R^{m\times s_q}$ extracts the previous input from the cache in $q$, so $u-E_{\rm prev}q$ is the input increment. Using the Lyapunov weight $\Pi$ for augmented-state tracking and $R,S\succeq0$ for input and increment penalties, with $\norm{e}_R^2=e^\top Re$ and $\norm{e}_S^2=e^\top Se$, define}
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\ell_t(q,u)={}&\norm{q-q_t^r}_\Pi^2+\norm{u-\vu_t^r}_R^2\\
&+\norm{(u-E_{\rm prev}q)-(\vu_t^r-\vu_{t-1}^r)}_S^2,
\end{split}
\label{eq:stage_cost}
\end{equation}
{\color{blue}With terminal weight $\beta\Pi$ ($\beta>0$), the frozen-LTV MPC problem is}

\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\min_{U}\quad
&\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\ell_{k+i}(p_{i|k},\vu_{i|k})
 +\beta\norm{p_{N|k}-q_{k+N}^r}_\Pi^2\\
\text{s.t.}\quad
&p_{0|k}=q_k^E,\\
&p_{i+1|k}=M_{i|k}p_{i|k}+D_{i|k}\vu_{i|k},\\
&\vu_{i|k}\in\mathcal U,\quad i=0,\ldots,N-1.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:quadratic_template}
\end{equation}

{\color{blue}The predicted states are determined by $U$ through these recursion constraints, so eliminating them gives the condensed QP in $U$ alone, with objective $J_k^{\rm fr}(U;\bar U_k)$; the second argument identifies the nominal sequence used to freeze the matrices. Let $W_k$ and $U_k^\star$ denote its optimal value and minimizer, so that $W_k=J_k^{\rm fr}(U_k^\star;\bar U_k)$. In condensed form the only remaining constraints are $(I_N\otimes H_u)U\le\mathbf1_N\otimes h_u$, where $\otimes$ denotes the Kronecker product. The set $\mathcal Q$ used in the analysis is not an MPC state constraint.}

{\color{blue}The condensed Hessian is positive definite even if $R$ or $S$ is singular: each nonzero input variation enters the augmented input cache and is penalized by $\Pi\succ0$ at a subsequent stage or by $\beta\Pi$ at the terminal step. Thus~\eqref{eq:quadratic_template} is a strictly convex QP over the nonempty compact set $\mathcal U^N$ (Appendix~\ref{app:estimates}).}

{\color{blue}Let $\widehat U_k\in\mathcal U^N$ be the feasible sequence returned by the QP solver, not necessarily the exact minimizer. The receding-horizon control law and nominal-sequence update are}
\begin{align}
\vu_k&=\widehat\vu_{0|k},\label{eq:apply_first}\\
\bar U_{k+1}&=(\widehat\vu_{1|k},\ldots,
\widehat\vu_{N-1|k},\vu_{k+N}^r).
\label{eq:nominal_shift}
\end{align}
{\color{blue}Algorithm~\ref{alg:mpc} summarizes the single-rollout, single-QP implementation.}

\begin{algorithm}[!t]
\caption{Single-Freeze Transformer--Koopman MPC}
\label{alg:mpc}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE {\color{blue}\textbf{Given:} deployed predictor, $\Pi$, $\mathcal U$, $N$, $\beta$, and reference.}
\STATE \textbf{Initialize:} {a feasible $\bar U_0\in\mathcal U^N$.}
\FOR{$k=0,1,\ldots$}
\STATE {\color{blue}Acquire $\vx_k$; update $\mX_k,v_k$ and form $q_k^E$ by~\eqref{eq:encoded_initialization}.}
\STATE {Set $\bar q_{0|k}=q_k^E$.}
\FOR{$i=0,\ldots,N-1$}
\STATE {\color{blue}Evaluate $\bar\mA_{i|k},\bar\mB_{i|k}$ at $\bar q_{i|k}$; form $M_{i|k},D_{i|k}$.}
\STATE {Propagate $\bar q_{i+1|k}=F(\bar q_{i|k},\bar\vu_{i|k})$.}
\ENDFOR
\STATE {\color{blue}Solve QP~\eqref{eq:quadratic_template} for a feasible $\widehat U_k$.}
\STATE {\color{blue}Apply $\vu_k=\widehat\vu_{0|k}$; update $\bar U_{k+1}$ by~\eqref{eq:nominal_shift}.}
\ENDFOR
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Closed-Loop Stability Analysis}
\label{subsec:closed_loop}
{\color{blue}Although each frozen MPC problem is convex, its prediction matrices change at the next sampling instant. Closed-loop analysis must therefore compare the optimal values of successive QPs using the feasible shift~\eqref{eq:nominal_shift}, while accounting for re-encoding, freezing, and reference mismatch. This comparison requires uniform bounds over all coefficient pairs generated in the analysis domain.}

\begin{assumption}[Common model and residual domain]
\label{ass:domain}
Fix the deployed model, $\Pi\succ0$, $M>0$, and $\mathcal Q=\{q:\norm{q}_\Pi\le M\}$, with $q_0^E\in\mathcal Q$. Let
\begin{equation}
\mathcal S_{\mathcal Q}=\{(\mA(\vc(\bar q)),\mB(C_xE_z^T\bar q)):
\bar q\in\mathcal Q\}.
\label{eq:frozen_family}
\end{equation}
For the pair generated at $\bar q\in\mathcal Q$, let $F^{\rm fr}(q,u)=Mq+Du$, with $M,D$ formed as in~\eqref{eq:frozen_model} from $\mA(\vc(\bar q))$ and $\mB(C_xE_z^T\bar q)$. For every $q,q'\in\mathcal Q$, $u\in\mathcal U$, and every such frozen map,
\begin{align}
\norm{F(q,u)-F(q',u)}_\Pi&\le\lambda\norm{q-q'}_\Pi,\nonumber\\
\norm{F^{\rm fr}(q,u)-F^{\rm fr}(q',u)}_\Pi&\le\lambda_{\rm fr}\norm{q-q'}_\Pi,\label{eq:common_contraction}\\
\norm{F(0,u)}_\Pi,\ \norm{F^{\rm fr}(0,u)}_\Pi&\le b_u,\nonumber\\
\norm{F(q,u)-F^{\rm fr}(q,u)}_\Pi&\le\bar\eta,\nonumber
\end{align}
where $0\le\lambda,\lambda_{\rm fr}<1$. For every admissible measured transition whose current encoded state lies in $\mathcal Q$, the residual in \eqref{eq:reencoding_residual} satisfies $\norm{w_k}_\Pi\le\bar w$. Moreover,
\begin{equation}
\lambda M+b_u+\bar w\le M,\qquad
\lambda_{\rm fr}M+b_u\le M.
\label{eq:domain_radius}
\end{equation}
All constants are uniform in time, nominal sequences, and candidate inputs.\envend
\end{assumption}

{Proposition~\ref{prop:contractive_class} gives sufficient structural choices for these constants, and Appendix~\ref{app:construction} supplies the amplitude bounds that yield a uniform $\bar\eta$. The re-encoding residual in~\eqref{eq:reencoding_residual} is not implied by the construction and remains a separate condition on the deployed lift and measured system.}

\begin{assumption}[Reference consistency]
\label{ass:reference}
{The prescribed reference satisfies the following conditions for every required time $t\ge0$, with a uniform bound $\bar d_{\rm ref}\ge0$:}
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
q_t^r\in\mathcal Q,\quad \vu_t^r\in\mathcal U,\quad
E_{\rm prev}q_t^r=\vu_{t-1}^r,\\
\norm{F(q_t^r,\vu_t^r)-q_{t+1}^r}_\Pi\le\bar d_{\rm ref}.
\end{split}
\label{eq:reference_defect}
\end{equation}
\envend
\end{assumption}

{\color{blue}At the reference input $u=\vu_t^r$, cache consistency gives $\ell_t(q,\vu_t^r)\le c_S\norm{q-q_t^r}_\Pi^2$, where $H_S=S^{1/2}E_{\rm prev}\Pi^{-1/2}$ and $c_S=1+\norm{H_S}_2^2$. Choose $\vartheta>0$ such that $(1+\vartheta)\lambda^2<1$ and}
\begin{equation}
\beta\ge\frac{c_S}{1-(1+\vartheta)\lambda^2}.
\label{eq:terminal_weight}
\end{equation}
{\color{blue}This ensures the terminal decrease inequality~\eqref{eq:tail_decrease} up to the reference-defect budget. Set $e_k=q_k^E-q_k^r$; the uniform constants $\varepsilon,\mathsf A,\mathsf B$ used below are specified in Appendices~\ref{app:estimates} and~\ref{app:comparison}.}

\begin{theorem}[Practical exponential tracking of single-freeze MPC]
\label{thm:tracking}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:domain}--\ref{ass:reference}, use \eqref{eq:terminal_weight} and execute Algorithm~\ref{alg:mpc} with exact input application. Suppose the returned sequence is primal feasible and satisfies
\begin{equation}
0\le J_k^{\rm fr}(\widehat U_k;\bar U_k)-W_k
\le\delta_k\le\bar\delta,
\label{eq:solver_gap}
\end{equation}
where $W_k$ is the optimal value in~\eqref{eq:quadratic_template}, and $\delta_k=0$ for an exact solve. Then all actual and predicted augmented states remain in $\mathcal Q$, and input feasibility is recursive. For $\rho=1-1/\mathsf A\in[0,1)$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\norm{e_k}_\Pi^2&\le W_k\le\Xi_k,\\
\Xi_k&=\mathsf A\rho^k\norm{e_0}_\Pi^2+\mathsf B\\
&+\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}\rho^{k-1-t}(\varepsilon+\delta_t).
\end{split}
\label{eq:squared_tracking_bound}
\end{equation}
In particular,
\begin{equation}
\norm{e_k}_\Pi\le\sqrt{\mathsf A}\rho^{k/2}\norm{e_0}_\Pi
+\sqrt{\mathsf B+\mathsf A(\varepsilon+\bar\delta)},
\label{eq:practical_tracking}
\end{equation}
and $\limsup_{k\to\infty}\norm{e_k}_\Pi\le
\sqrt{\mathsf B+\mathsf A(\varepsilon+\bar\delta)}$.
If $\bar w=\bar\eta=\bar d_{\rm ref}=\bar\delta=0$, the tracking error converges exponentially to zero.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For a current point in $\mathcal Q$, the complete next-state norm is at most $\lambda M+b_u$, and the frozen bound is $\lambda_{\rm fr}M+b_u$. Equation~\eqref{eq:domain_radius} first keeps the nominal rollout in $\mathcal Q$, so its coefficient pairs belong to $\mathcal S_{\mathcal Q}$. Induction then keeps every complete and frozen candidate in the domain. Adding the residual bound $\bar w$ keeps the next actual initialization in $\mathcal Q$. The returned sequence is feasible for the constraint set of~\eqref{eq:quadratic_template}, and its shift remains feasible because the reference tail belongs to $\mathcal U$.

For every candidate first input, the complete and frozen updates coincide because both use coefficients evaluated at $q_k^E$. Along the nominal input sequence, induction gives equality of the complete and frozen trajectories at every prediction step.

For the returned sequence at sample $k$, write $p_i=p_{i|k}$ and $\widehat\vu_i=\widehat\vu_{i|k}$, and set $U^+=\bar U_{k+1}$ as in~\eqref{eq:nominal_shift}. First-step equality and~\eqref{eq:reencoding_residual} give $q_{k+1}^E=p_1+w_k$. Let $\widetilde q_j$ denote the complete trajectory from $q_{k+1}^E$ along $U^+$. Its initial distance from $p_1$ is at most $d_0=\bar w$, and the intermediate bounds satisfy $d_{j+1}=\lambda d_j+\bar\eta$ for $0\le j<N-1$. With $d_j$ specified by~\eqref{eq:shift_error},
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\norm{\widetilde q_j-p_{j+1}}_\Pi&\le d_j,\qquad 0\le j<N,\\
\norm{\widetilde q_N-F(p_N,\vu_{k+N}^r)}_\Pi&\le\lambda d_{N-1}.
\end{split}
\label{eq:shift_comparison}
\end{equation}
At the reference tail, both comparison points are advanced by the same complete map $F$ under $\vu_{k+N}^r$, giving the factor $\lambda$ in the second line.

The reference input also bounds the tail cost: its input-error term vanishes, and its relative-increment cost is at most $(c_S-1)\norm{q-q_t^r}_\Pi^2$. Contraction, Young's inequality, and~\eqref{eq:terminal_weight} therefore give
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\ell_t(q,\vu_t^r)+\beta\norm{F(q,\vu_t^r)-q_{t+1}^r}_\Pi^2
-\beta\norm{q-q_t^r}_\Pi^2\le\tau_{\rm ref},
\end{split}
\label{eq:tail_decrease}
\end{equation}
where $\tau_{\rm ref}$ is defined in Appendix~\ref{app:estimates}. Since $U^+$ is the next nominal sequence, its frozen candidate trajectory equals $\widetilde q$. At matched absolute times and inputs, the continuity bounds~\eqref{eq:cost_continuity} convert the trajectory differences into the budget $\varepsilon$ in~\eqref{eq:epsilon_closed_loop}. Applying~\eqref{eq:tail_decrease} at the old endpoint $p_N$ yields
\begin{align}
W_{k+1}&\le J_{k+1}^{\rm fr}(U^+;U^+)\nonumber\\
&\le J_k^{\rm fr}(\widehat U_k;\bar U_k)
-\ell_k(q_k^E,\widehat\vu_{0|k})+\varepsilon\nonumber\\
&\le W_k-\norm{e_k}_\Pi^2+\varepsilon+\delta_k.
\label{eq:value_descent}
\end{align}
This comparison uses the next sample's prescribed nominal rollout and the exact optimal values of the successive frozen QPs.

The initial stage cost gives $W_k\ge\norm{e_k}_\Pi^2$. For an upper bound, evaluate the QP objective at the feasible reference input sequence $(\vu_k^r,\ldots,\vu_{k+N-1}^r)$. Contraction and the frozen-model discrepancy~\eqref{eq:frozen_discrepancy} bound its state error by $\lambda^i\norm{e_k}_\Pi+b_i$, with $b_i$ defined in Appendix~\ref{app:comparison}. The input-error terms vanish. After the first step, the previous-input slot matches the reference, so only the initial relative-increment term remains; it is at most $\norm{H_S}_2^2\norm{e_k}_\Pi^2$. Squaring and summing the state and terminal estimates gives
\begin{equation}
\norm{e_k}_\Pi^2\le W_k\le\mathsf A\norm{e_k}_\Pi^2+\mathsf B.
\label{eq:value_comparison}
\end{equation}
Substituting~\eqref{eq:value_comparison} into~\eqref{eq:value_descent} gives $W_{k+1}\le\rho W_k+\mathsf B/\mathsf A+\varepsilon+\delta_k$. Expanding this recursion and using $W_0\le\mathsf A\norm{e_0}_\Pi^2+\mathsf B$ yields $W_k\le\Xi_k$. The lower comparison then proves~\eqref{eq:squared_tracking_bound}. Bounding the geometric sum by $\mathsf A$ and taking square roots gives~\eqref{eq:practical_tracking} and its limiting radius. When all four error budgets vanish, $\mathsf B=\varepsilon=\bar\delta=0$, which gives exponential convergence to zero.
\end{proof}

{Since $\vx_k=C_mq_k^E$ with $C_m=C_xE_z^T$, the augmented-state bound projects to measured coordinates; if $R\succ0$, it also bounds the applied input error.}

\begin{corollary}[Measured and input tracking]
\label{cor:projections}
{Under the conditions of Theorem~\ref{thm:tracking}, the measurement error satisfies}
\begin{equation}
\norm{\vx_k-C_mq_k^r}_2\le\norm{C_m\Pi^{-1/2}}_2\norm{e_k}_\Pi.
\label{eq:measurement_tracking}
\end{equation}
If $R\succ0$, then
\begin{equation}
\norm{\widehat\vu_{0|k}-\vu_k^r}_2^2\le
\frac{\Xi_k+\delta_k}{\lambda_{\min}(R)}.
\label{eq:input_tracking}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Since $\vx_k=C_mq_k^E$, the induced norm gives~\eqref{eq:measurement_tracking}. For $R\succ0$, the squared input error times $\lambda_{\min}(R)$ is at most the returned cost. By~\eqref{eq:solver_gap} and~\eqref{eq:squared_tracking_bound}, $J_k^{\rm fr}(\widehat U_k;\bar U_k)\le W_k+\delta_k\le\Xi_k+\delta_k$. Division by $\lambda_{\min}(R)$ gives~\eqref{eq:input_tracking}.
\end{proof}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
This paper developed a history-conditioned scheduled Koopman predictor with a measurement-preserving temporal Transformer lift and bounded low-rank transition and input modulation. Nonredundant measurement and input caches define the recursively propagated augmented state. A Schur skeleton and explicit schedule-derivative budgets provide a constructible common-contraction model class without removing nonconstant history dependence.

For control, one nominal rollout and one frozen convex QP are coupled by a reference-relative augmented tracking objective and a shift of the returned sequence with a reference tail. A direct comparison of successive frozen optimal values establishes practical exponential tracking under uniform contraction, re-encoding residual, reference-defect, and feasible objective-gap conditions. The resulting radius separates these error contributions and projects directly to measured coordinates. The guarantee is for the specified single-freeze controller and model class; it is not an unconditional claim about an arbitrary learned predictor or hidden physical state.

\appendices
\section{Contractive Predictor Construction}
\label{app:construction}
The skeleton in \eqref{eq:lyapunov_skeleton} is block triangular, with eigenvalues those of $\mA_0$ and the nilpotent shift blocks. Thus it is Schur, and $\Pi=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(M_0^T)^jM_0^j$ is positive definite. Its induced norm satisfies $\lambda_0^2=1-1/\lambda_{\max}(\Pi)<1$.

For completeness, the softmax Jacobian $J(\pi)=\operatorname{diag}(\pi)-\pi\pi^T$ has Euclidean norm at most $1/2$. Combining this bound, the $1$-Lipschitz property of $\tanh$, and $\norm{b}_1\le\sqrt r\norm{b}_2$ gives \eqref{eq:Lipschitz_constants}: split the change of $\pi_j\tanh([Wy+b]_j)$ into the activation change and the softmax-weight change, then sum the unit-bounded rank-one factors. The first sum is at most $\norm{W}_2\norm{\Delta y}_2$, the second at most $\sqrt r\norm{W^\pi}_2\norm{\Delta y}_2/2$.

Let $L_c$ bound the context derivative on the selected ball. A two-layer $\tanh$ context has $L_c\le\norm{W_{c,2}}_2\norm{W_{c,1}}_2$. For a single attention head $Y(Z)=P(Z)V(Z)W_O$, $P=\operatorname{softmax}_{\rm row}(Q(Z)K(Z)^T/\sqrt{d_h})$, suppose the affine query, key, and value maps have Frobenius amplitude bounds $M_Q,M_K,M_V$ and Lipschitz constants $L_Q,L_K,L_V$ on a convex token domain. With $L_{\rm tok}$ tokens,
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
L_{\rm score}&\le(L_QM_K+M_QL_K)/\sqrt{d_h},\\
L_Y&\le\norm{W_O}_2\left(\tfrac12M_VL_{\rm score}
+\sqrt{L_{\rm tok}}L_V\right).
\end{split}
\label{eq:attention_bound}
\end{equation}
This follows from the product rule, the rowwise softmax bound, and $\norm{P}_2\le\norm{P}_F\le\sqrt{L_{\rm tok}}$. Multihead concatenation combines the head bounds in Euclidean norm before the final projection. Residual blocks have constant at most one plus the branch constant; feedforward and embedding constants multiply under composition. Layer normalization with fixed positive $\epsilon_{\rm LN}$ has derivative norm at most $\norm{\gamma_{\rm LN}}_\infty/\sqrt{\epsilon_{\rm LN}}$, and mean pooling has norm $1/\sqrt{L_{\rm tok}}$. Fixed positions and biases enter amplitude bounds. These formulas permit constrained Transformer context maps, but do not imply that an unconstrained trained network has a useful small bound. When the temporal encoder uses GELU activations, its feature-map bound additionally incorporates a uniform activation-derivative bound.

Define the norm-conversion factors and domain bounds
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
a_z&=\norm{\Pi^{1/2}E_z}_2,\quad
b_z=\norm{E_z^T\Pi^{-1/2}}_2,\\
b_q&=\norm{\Pi^{-1/2}}_2,\quad
b_x=\norm{C_xE_z^T\Pi^{-1/2}}_2,\\
M_u&=\sup_{u\in\mathcal U}\norm{u}_2,\qquad M_z=b_zM.
\end{split}
\label{eq:norm_conversion}
\end{equation}
For a variation $\Delta q$, with $\Delta\vz=E_z^T\Delta q$, the top block of $D_qF-M_0$ is the sum
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
&(\mA(\vc)-\mA_0)\Delta\vz
+D\mA(\vc)[D\vc(q)\Delta q]\vz\\
&\hspace{14mm}+D\mB(C_x\vz)[C_x\Delta\vz]u.
\end{split}
\label{eq:full_derivative}
\end{equation}
The other blocks vanish. Therefore
\begin{equation}
\nu_{\rm bd}=a_z(g_A b_z+L_A L_cM_zb_q+L_BM_ub_x)
\label{eq:derivative_budget}
\end{equation}
bounds its $\Pi$-norm. The frozen perturbation is bounded by $a_zg_A b_z$, uniformly over all coefficients generated in the ball. Smoothness on the ball and its line segments turns these derivative bounds into same-input Lipschitz bounds.

To give a fully specified gain choice, let
\begin{equation*}
b_0^{\rm drv}=\sup_{u\in\mathcal U}
\norm{\Pi^{1/2}\col(\mB_0u,0,E_uu)}_2.
\end{equation*}
Choose a trial ball satisfying~\eqref{eq:radius_margin}. Fix $\bar\kappa_A,\bar\kappa_B,\bar L_c$ so that $\kappa_\chi\le\bar\kappa_\chi$ and $L_c\le\bar L_c$ throughout the allowed parameter set on this ball. Take $g_A=t\widehat g_A$, $g_B=t\widehat g_B$ with $\widehat g_A,\widehat g_B>0$, and set
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
k_F&=a_z(\widehat g_A b_z+\widehat g_A\bar\kappa_A\bar L_c b_zMb_q
+\widehat g_B\bar\kappa_BM_ub_x),\\
k_{\rm fr}&=a_z\widehat g_A b_z,\quad k_b=a_z\widehat g_BM_u,
\quad k_* =\max(k_F,k_{\rm fr}).
\end{split}
\label{eq:gain_coefficients}
\end{equation}
Any choice
\begin{equation}
0<t<\min\left\{1,\frac{1-\lambda_0}{2k_*},
\frac{\Delta}{2(k_*M+k_b)}\right\}
\label{eq:gain_choice}
\end{equation}
works, omitting zero-denominator terms. Indeed, $\nu_{\rm bd}\le tk_F$ uniformly over the allowed parameters, so $\lambda\le\lambda_0+tk_F$, $\lambda_{\rm fr}\le\lambda_0+tk_{\rm fr}$, and $b_u\le b_0^{\rm drv}+tk_b$. The selected gains are then fixed for the second training stage. The contraction increment uses less than half the original rate margin, while $t(k_*M+k_b)<\Delta/2$ leaves a strict radius margin. The ball is chosen before the gains; a residual bound available only on a smaller set therefore cannot justify enlarging it. For arbitrary complete and frozen points in it, subtracting the top blocks gives the uniform mismatch bound $a_z(2g_AM_z+2g_BM_u)$ used in the analysis.

A nontrivial member of the mathematical class takes $n=m=1$, $P_h=2$, $\vz=(x,\psi)$, $\psi(X)=\alpha_{\rm d} x_{\rm past}$, and $\alpha_{\rm d}\ne0$. Set $c=a+v$ and
\begin{equation}
\mA(c)=\begin{bmatrix}a_0+g_A\tanh c&0\\\alpha_{\rm d}&0\end{bmatrix},\quad
\mB(x)=\begin{bmatrix}b_0+g_B\tanh x\\0\end{bmatrix},
\label{eq:analytic_example}
\end{equation}
where $|a_0|<1$, $b_0\ne0$, and both positive gains obey \eqref{eq:gain_choice}. These are rank-one corrections with genuine history and measurement dependence. Since $\psi^+=\alpha_{\rm d}x=\alpha_{\rm d}a^+$, the encoding graph is preserved. On an input box containing zero in its interior, a small alternating input $\vu_t^r=h(-1)^t$ has a unique periodic state in the already invariant ball: its two-step map is a contraction. At $h=0$, its linearization gives $x_t^r=-b_0h(-1)^t/(1+a_0)+o(h)$, so the measurement is nonconstant for sufficiently small nonzero $h$. This delay-coordinate example establishes nonemptiness of the model class, not exact representation or encoding consistency of an arbitrary Transformer.

Finally, the actual residual interface can be decomposed without assuming invariance of the encoder image. At an encoded initial point, let $\widehat\vz$ be the predicted next lift, $\widehat x=C_x\widehat\vz$, and $\widetilde X=\operatorname{shift}(\mX,\widehat x)$. If $C_\psi$ selects the learned feature block and $E_X$ appends a window-end perturbation, define
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\nu&=x^+-\widehat x,\qquad
\kappa=\psi_\theta(\widetilde X)-C_\psi\widehat\vz,\\
r&=\col\bigl(\nu,\kappa+
\psi_\theta(\widetilde X+E_X\nu)-\psi_\theta(\widetilde X)\bigr).
\end{split}
\label{eq:encoded_residual_decomposition}
\end{equation}
With exact caches, $w=\col(r,0,0)$. Uniform bounds $\bar\nu,\bar\kappa$ and a feature Lipschitz constant $L_\psi$ on all these windows give the sufficient budget $\bar w=a_z\sqrt{\bar\nu^2+(\bar\kappa+L_\psi\bar\nu)^2}$. The two terms measure physical measurement-prediction error and encoding-graph defect, respectively; neither is supplied by the gain construction alone.

\section{Technical Estimates for Frozen MPC}
\label{app:estimates}
For the condensed QP, stack $P=\col(p_{0|k},\ldots,p_{N|k})=P_0+\mathcal T U$. Let $K_pP=\col(E_{\rm prev}p_{0|k},\ldots,E_{\rm prev}p_{N-1|k})$, $\mathcal D_\Delta=I-K_p\mathcal T$, $Q_d=\operatorname{diag}(\Pi,\ldots,\Pi,\beta\Pi)$, $R_d=I_N\otimes R$, and $S_d=I_N\otimes S$. The Hessian of \eqref{eq:quadratic_template} is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal H=2(\mathcal T^TQ_d\mathcal T+R_d+
\mathcal D_\Delta^TS_d\mathcal D_\Delta)\succeq0.
\label{eq:QP_Hessian}
\end{equation}
Domain invariance follows from \eqref{eq:domain_radius} in the order established in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:tracking}: nominal rollout, frozen candidates, and then the next actual initialization. No estimate presupposes future membership.

For the strict convexity claim, let $\Delta U\ne0$ and let $j$ be its first nonzero block. Frozen causality gives $\Delta p_i=0$ for $i\le j$, while the last input-cache block of $\Delta p_{j+1}$ is $\Delta u_j\ne0$. If $j+1<N$, its state quadratic is weighted by $\Pi\succ0$; if $j+1=N$, it is weighted by $\beta\Pi\succ0$. Every other quadratic term is nonnegative. Thus the second variation is strictly positive. This argument neither assumes controllability of the lifted measurement block nor requires $R\succ0$.

Let $f_i$ and $p_i$ be full and frozen predictions for the same arbitrary candidate sequence. Both start at the point used for the first nominal coefficient evaluation, giving $f_0=p_0$ and $f_1=p_1$. Thereafter,
\begin{equation*}
\norm{f_{i+1}-p_{i+1}}_\Pi\le
\lambda\norm{f_i-p_i}_\Pi+\bar\eta,
\end{equation*}
by inserting $F(p_i,u_i)$. Hence
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\norm{f_i-p_i}_\Pi&\le D_i,\qquad D_0=D_1=0,\\
D_i&=\bar\eta\sum_{s=0}^{i-2}\lambda^s,\qquad i\ge2.
\end{split}
\label{eq:frozen_discrepancy}
\end{equation}
For the nominal input sequence, induction gives identical full and frozen state sequences. The shifted comparison \eqref{eq:shift_comparison} uses the recurrence for $j<N-1$ and two complete updates at the reference tail, giving $\lambda d_{N-1}$.

Throughout the analysis, empty sums are zero and $\lambda^0=1$. The uniform shift bounds are
\begin{equation}
d_j=\lambda^j\bar w+\bar\eta\sum_{s=0}^{j-1}\lambda^s,\qquad 0\le j<N.
\label{eq:shift_error}
\end{equation}
With $\tau_{\rm ref}=\beta(1+1/\vartheta)\bar d_{\rm ref}^2$ and $L_\ell,L_f$ from \eqref{eq:cost_continuity}, the cost budget is
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon=\tau_{\rm ref}+L_\ell\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}d_j+L_f\lambda d_{N-1}.
\label{eq:epsilon_closed_loop}
\end{equation}
These constants are uniform. For $N=1$, there are no intermediate shift steps, and~\eqref{eq:epsilon_closed_loop} reduces to $\varepsilon=\tau_{\rm ref}+(L_\ell+\lambda L_f)\bar w$.

To derive \eqref{eq:tail_decrease}, put $e=q-q_t^r$. At $u=\vu_t^r$, the $R$ term is zero and the weighted relative increment is $-S^{1/2}E_{\rm prev}e$, so $\ell_t\le c_S\norm{e}_\Pi^2$. By contraction and Young's inequality,
\begin{equation*}
\norm{F(q,\vu_t^r)-q_{t+1}^r}_\Pi^2
\le(1+\vartheta)\lambda^2\norm{e}_\Pi^2
+(1+1/\vartheta)\bar d_{\rm ref}^2.
\end{equation*}
The coefficient of $\norm{e}_\Pi^2$ after adding the stage cost and subtracting the old terminal cost is $c_S-\beta[1-(1+\vartheta)\lambda^2]\le0$. For an exact model reference, $\bar d_{\rm ref}=0$, direct contraction uses $\lambda^2$ without Young's inequality. The tail bound~\eqref{eq:tail_decrease} then holds with $\tau_{\rm ref}=0$ under the sharper condition $\beta\ge c_S/(1-\lambda^2)$.

The explicit uniform cost bounds used in \eqref{eq:epsilon_closed_loop} are
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
D_u^S&=\sup_{u,v\in\mathcal U}\norm{S^{1/2}(u-v)}_2,\quad
T_\Delta=D_u^S+2M\norm{H_S}_2,\\
L_\ell&=4M+2T_\Delta\norm{H_S}_2,\qquad L_f=4\beta M.
\end{split}
\label{eq:cost_continuity}
\end{equation}
For their derivation, errors relative to $q_t^r\in\mathcal Q$ have norm at most $2M$. Their squared-norm difference is at most $4M\norm{q-q'}_\Pi$. The weighted increment vector is
\begin{equation*}
S^{1/2}(u-\vu_t^r)-H_S\Pi^{1/2}(q-q_t^r),
\end{equation*}
whose norm is at most $T_\Delta$ and whose difference at a common input is at most $\norm{H_S}_2\norm{q-q'}_\Pi$. Thus its squared-norm difference is at most $2T_\Delta\norm{H_S}_2\norm{q-q'}_\Pi$. The $R$ term cancels, and the terminal estimate multiplies the state estimate by $\beta$, proving \eqref{eq:cost_continuity}.

\section{Value Comparison and Feasible Solver Error}
\label{app:comparison}
For $D_i$ in \eqref{eq:frozen_discrepancy}, define the reference/frozen error accumulation and the comparison constants by
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
b_i&=\bar d_{\rm ref}\sum_{s=0}^{i-1}\lambda^s+D_i,\qquad 0\le i\le N,\\
\mathsf A&=2\left(\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\lambda^{2i}+\beta\lambda^{2N}\right)
+\norm{H_S}_2^2,\\
\mathsf B&=2\left(\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}b_i^2+\beta b_N^2\right).
\end{split}
\label{eq:value_constants}
\end{equation}
The constants are uniform over all allowed nominal sequences; $\mathsf A\ge2$ gives $\rho=1-1/\mathsf A\in[0,1)$.

Consider the full trajectory for the reference input candidate from an arbitrary current state. Its reference error $r_i$ satisfies $r_{i+1}\le\lambda r_i+\bar d_{\rm ref}$, hence $r_i\le\lambda^i\norm{e_k}_\Pi+\bar d_{\rm ref}\sum_{s=0}^{i-1}\lambda^s$. Adding the full/frozen discrepancy $D_i$ gives the bound $\lambda^i\norm{e_k}_\Pi+b_i$. The reference candidate has zero input-error cost. For $i\ge1$ its predicted previous-input slot is exactly $\vu_{k+i-1}^r$, so the relative-increment cost is also zero; only the initial term, at most $\norm{H_S}_2^2\norm{e_k}_\Pi^2$, remains. Applying $(a+b)^2\le2a^2+2b^2$ to every state and terminal error yields \eqref{eq:value_comparison} with exactly \eqref{eq:value_constants}.

To expose the cost cancellation in \eqref{eq:value_descent}, write $g=F(p_N,\vu_{k+N}^r)$. The matched stages and the new terminal term satisfy
\begin{align*}
J_{k+1}^{\rm fr}(U^+;U^+)&\le\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\ell_{k+i}(p_i,\widehat\vu_i)\\
&+\ell_{k+N}(p_N,\vu_{k+N}^r)\\
&+\beta\norm{g-q_{k+N+1}^r}_\Pi^2\\
&+L_\ell\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}d_j+L_f\lambda d_{N-1}\\
&\le J_k^{\rm fr}(\widehat U_k;\bar U_k)
-\ell_k(p_0,\widehat\vu_0)+\varepsilon.
\end{align*}
The sum is empty for $N=1$. General cache perturbations are already covered by the state continuity estimates; no equality of all caches is used.

The comparison bound is for the exact value $W_k$ of each nominal-dependent QP, even when its delivered sequence is suboptimal. Combining the gap \eqref{eq:solver_gap} with the preceding inequality adds $\delta_k$, not $\delta_k+\delta_{k+1}$. Expanding the resulting scalar recursion gives
\begin{equation*}
W_k\le\rho^kW_0+\mathsf B(1-\rho^k)
+\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}\rho^{k-1-t}(\varepsilon+\delta_t).
\end{equation*}
Using $W_0\le\mathsf A\norm{e_0}_\Pi^2+\mathsf B$ gives $W_k\le\Xi_k$ with $\Xi_k$ defined in \eqref{eq:squared_tracking_bound}; the lower comparison then bounds the state error. In particular, the limiting input radius from \eqref{eq:input_tracking} is at most $\sqrt{[\mathsf B+\mathsf A(\varepsilon+\bar\delta)+\bar\delta]/\lambda_{\min}(R)}$ when $R\succ0$. A singular $R$ cannot supply this Euclidean input bound by division, although the state theorem and strict convexity remain valid.

A feasible output and a dual-feasible lower value $L_k\le W_k$ from the same QP give the valid gap bound $J_k^{\rm fr}(\widehat U_k)-L_k$; obtaining such a certificate does not require a second QP. More explicitly, for $J(U)=\tfrac12U^T\mathcal HU+g^TU+c$, constraints $G_UU\le h$, a certified $\mathcal H\succeq\mu I\succ0$, and $y\ge0$, let $r_{\rm stat}=\mathcal H\widehat U+g+G_U^Ty$. Strong convexity of the Lagrangian gives
\begin{equation*}
J(\widehat U)-W\le\frac{\norm{r_{\rm stat}}_2^2}{2\mu}
+y^T(h-G_U\widehat U).
\end{equation*}
Feasibility, $\mu$, and residual calculations must be valid under the numerical error model used. The theorem does not assert that a particular iteration limit or solver automatically meets these conditions, and an unanalysed infeasible fallback or input modification is outside the specified control law.
